Consecutive integers free of certain prime factors

Wouter van Doorn, Quanyu Tang

Erdős conjectured that you can find a run of consecutive integers where none is divisible by any 'medium-sized' prime — specifically, primes between k and 2k — and that such runs first appear surprisingly late. This paper confirms that conjecture, proving the first such run of length k doesn't appear until n exceeds a doubly-logarithmic exponential tower, a super-polynomial bound. The result advances our understanding of how prime factors distribute across consecutive integers.

math.NT
Original abstract

Let $n_k$ denote the least integer $n>2k$ such that $(n-k)(n-k+1)\cdots(n-1)$ is not divisible by any prime in the interval $(k,2k)$. Confirming a conjecture of Erdős, we prove that, for all sufficiently large $k$, $$ n_k > e^{\frac{\log^2 k}{20 \log \log k}}. $$

Randomly piercing algebraic sets

Daniel Altman, Nathan Tung

How many random points in the finite vector space 𝔽ₚⁿ are needed to guarantee hitting every quadratic hypersurface (the zero set of a degree-2 polynomial)? This paper finds a sharp threshold: roughly n² · log(p)/2log(1+1/(p-1)) points suffice, and fewer almost surely miss some hypersurface. Sharp thresholds like this, common in random graph theory, are rare in algebraic geometry over finite fields.

math.NT math.CO
Original abstract

We show, for example, that if one samples \[\frac{\log p}{2\log(1+(p-1)^{-1})} \cdot n^2(1 + o_{n\to \infty}(1))\] points in $\mathbb{F}_p^n$ at random then asymptotically almost surely this set intersects every quadratic hypersurface. We furthermore show that this is tight in that sampling $o_{n\to\infty}(n^2)$ fewer points almost surely fails to intersect some quadratic hypersurface. Our main result is a sharp threshold for the following problem: how many points in $\mathbb{F}_p^n$ does one need to randomly sample to almost surely intersect every algebraic set defined by at most $s$ polynomials each of degree at most $k$? As an application we improve lower bounds in the random Szemerédi theorem in $\mathbb{F}_p^n$, in particular obtaining a leading constant which grows as the threshold for what is considered a `dense' set in Szemerédi's theorem shrinks.

Modular Heights of Unitary Shimura Varieties III: Proof of the Main Theorem

Ziqi Guo

Shimura varieties are geometric spaces encoding deep arithmetic information, and their 'modular height' measures arithmetic complexity. This paper — the third in a series — proves a formula expressing that height for unitary Shimura varieties over CM fields in terms of logarithmic derivatives of Hecke L-functions, linking arithmetic geometry directly to analytic number theory via a novel comparison of Eisenstein-theta series and arithmetic intersection theory.

math.NT
Original abstract

This is the third and the last of a series of three papers, in which we prove a formula expressing the modular height of a unitary Shimura variety over a CM number field in terms of the logarithmic derivative of the Hecke L-function associated with the CM extension. The main idea of our proof is to compare the holomorphic projection of the derivative of a certain mixed Eisenstein-theta series and the arithmetic degree of a generating series of divisors on unitary Shimura varieties. In this paper, we compute the arithmetic degree of the arithmetic generating series of divisors on unitary Shimura varieties, and then, combining with the results from the first two papers in this series, derive the modular height formula for unitary Shimura varieties.

Small values of Carmichael's lambda function

Paul Pollack

Carmichael's lambda function λ(n) gives the largest order of any element in the multiplicative group mod n — a fundamental quantity in number theory and cryptography. This paper proves a sharp upper bound on how often λ(n) is unusually small, valid across a wide range, and conditionally shows the bound is tight. As a consequence, it sharpens estimates on how rarely the multiplicative order of 2 modulo n is much smaller than expected.

math.NT
Original abstract

Let $λ(n)$ be the exponent of the multiplicative group $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}$, and set $L(x,y) = \#\{n\le x: λ(n)\le y\}$. We prove an upper bound for $\log \frac{L(x,y)}{x}$ valid for $\exp((\log_2{x})^{1+ε}) \le y\le x/\exp((\log_2{x})^{1+ε})$. Our bound is asymptotically sharp under a plausible hypothesis on powersmooth shifted primes. As an application, we obtain a new upper bound on the count of odd $n\le x$ for which the order of $2$ modulo $n$ is appreciably smaller than $x^{1/2}$.

Modular Heights of Unitary Shimura Varieties II: Arithmetic Generating Series of Divisors

Ziqi Guo

Shimura varieties are geometric objects encoding deep number-theoretic symmetries, and their 'height' measures arithmetic complexity. This paper, part of a series, establishes a precise formula linking the modular height of unitary Shimura varieties to the logarithmic derivative of a Hecke L-function—a central object in analytic number theory—by comparing two independently constructed generating series of arithmetic divisors.

Original abstract

This is the second of a series of three papers, in which we prove a formula expressing the modular height of a unitary Shimura variety over a CM number field in terms of the logarithmic derivative of the Hecke L-function associated with the CM extension. The main idea of our proof is to compare the holomorphic projection of the derivative of a certain mixed Eisenstein-theta series and the arithmetic degree of a generating series of divisors on unitary Shimura varieties. Factorizations of linearized polynomials and extremal curves in odd characteristic

Tetsushi Ito, Daichi Takeuchi, Takahiro Tsushima

Extremal curves over finite fields achieve the maximum possible number of points relative to their genus, making them rare and valuable in coding theory and arithmetic geometry. This paper gives an explicit construction of all extremal van der Geer–van der Vlugt curves in odd characteristic using linearized polynomials—polynomials whose roots form a vector space—extending a previously known characteristic-two recipe to complete the picture.

math.NT math.AG
Original abstract

We give a complete recipe for constructing extremal van der Geer--van der Vlugt curves over finite fields of odd characteristic. The input data consist of a nonzero element of the base field together with a linear subspace satisfying a certain trace condition. This construction may be viewed as an odd-characteristic analogue of the one previously obtained by the authors in characteristic two.

On the classification of indecomposable Ekedahl-Oort strata in unitary Shimura varieties, and related Newton polygons

Emerald Andrews, Deewang Bhamidipati, Maria Fox et al.

Shimura varieties are geometric objects encoding arithmetic data about abelian varieties, and Ekedahl-Oort strata stratify them by the p-torsion structure of those varieties in characteristic p. This paper completely classifies the 'indecomposable' strata for unitary Shimura varieties, organizing them into four explicit types—two classical and two arising from a construction of Serre—and provides an algorithm connecting combinatorial descriptions to geometric Newton polygons.

math.NT
Original abstract

In this paper, we give a complete classification of indecomposable Ekedahl-Oort strata of Shimura varieties associated to the unitary group $\mathsf{GU}(a, b)$ over an odd inert prime. We show that each indecomposable stratum is one of four types: unitary unicycle, unitary bicycle, Serre unicycle, or Serre bicycle; the latter two types are named for a tensor construction of abelian varieties developed by Serre. We provide an algorithm that translates the description of a stratum in terms of words in the alphabet $\{\texttt{f},\texttt{v}\}$ to the corresponding Weyl group coset representative. Finally, using a $p$-adic lift, we construct a `tautological' point in each Ekedahl-Oort stratum, and compute its Newton polygon. As an application, we show that the indecomposable Ekedahl-Oort strata corresponding to unitary unicycles and Serre unicycles always intersect the supersingular locus.

Original abstract

We disprove the near-quadratic Elekes--Rónyai expander conjecture over $\mathbb R$. Quantitative Oppenheim Conjecture for Random Quadratic Forms and Optimal Variance Bounds in Function Fields

Jiyoung Han, Noy Soffer Aranov

Oppenheim's conjecture asks whether irrational indefinite quadratic forms take values arbitrarily close to zero on integer lattices — here the authors prove a quantitative version in the function field setting, counting how often this happens. They compute higher moments of the Siegel transform (a tool converting lattice-point counts into integrals) and obtain optimal variance bounds, meaning the fluctuations in lattice-point counts are pinned down as tightly as possible.

math.NT math.DS math.PR
Original abstract

We prove a quantitative version of Oppenheim's conjecture in the function field setting. In order to do so, we compute the higher moments of the Siegel transform. In particular, we find an optimal bound on the variance of the number of lattice points in a set. Moreover, we compute the exact variance of the number of lattice points in a ball, which is of independent interest.

Indivisibility of ray class groups of real quadratic fields

Emmanuel Lecouturier, Christian Maire

Ray class groups are refined versions of ideal class groups that encode arithmetic structure of number fields with prescribed congruence conditions. This paper shows that for real quadratic fields where a prime ℓ splits, the 'minus part' of the ray class group is frequently not divisible by a related prime p, and quantifies exactly how many such fields exist up to a given bound. The result is unconditional when ℓ = 2p+1, addressing a longstanding question about indivisibility statistics in real quadratic fields.

math.NT
Original abstract

Let ${\ell}$, p $\ge$ 5 be primes such that p | (${\ell}$ -1). Let $Δ$ > 0 be the fundamental discriminant of a real quadratic field in which ${\ell}$ splits. We denote by h - ${\ell}$ ($Δ$) the order of the minus part (for the Galois action) of the ray class group of Q( $\sqrt$ $Δ$) of modulus ${\ell}$. In this paper, we study the indivisibility of h - ${\ell}$ ($Δ$) by p, and prove that under the assumption that this set is non-empty. This lower bound is made unconditional if ${\ell}$ = 2p + 1, i.e. if p is a Sophie Germain prime. Our result can be viewed as being in the continuity of the results of Kohnen-Ono, Ono, Byeon, Beckwith etc. regarding the class numbers of quadratic fields, in the sense that we rely on techniques from the theory of half-integral weight modular forms. Significant difficulties however arise in our study, as we have to study Eisenstein congruences for cuspforms of weight 3 2 , and use a generalized Shimura correspondence of Baruch-Mao. Combined with the results of Lecouturier-Wang, our result has implications eg. for the 5-part of BSD for even quadratic twists of X 0 (11).

Square-root cancellation for sums of coefficients of $\operatorname{GL}(m)$-automorphic forms over values of random polynomials

Dimitrios Chatzakos, Efthymios Sofos

How randomly do the Fourier coefficients of automorphic forms—functions central to modern number theory—oscillate when sampled along polynomial sequences? This paper proves they exhibit 'square-root cancellation,' meaning their sum over N points is roughly √N rather than N, the gold standard of pseudorandom behavior. This extends classical results to higher-rank GL(m) forms and random polynomial inputs simultaneously, tackling a frontier case where both the arithmetic complexity and the sampling set are hard.

math.NT
Original abstract

For arbitrary $m\geq 2$ and $d\geq 1$, we establish square-root cancellation for sums of Fourier coefficients of $\mathrm{GL}(m)$-automorphic forms averaged over values of random degree-$d$ polynomials.

Large values of quadratic Dirichlet $L$-functions of prime-related moduli

Peng Gao

Dirichlet $L$-functions are generalizations of the Riemann zeta function encoding number-theoretic data, and their central values are of great arithmetic interest. Assuming the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, this paper uses the 'resonator method' to show these central values can be unexpectedly large for quadratic $L$-functions with prime-related moduli. Such large-value results probe the extremes of $L$-function distributions and sharpen our understanding of their statistical behavior.

math.NT
Original abstract

In this paper, we apply the long resonator method to exhibit large values at the central point of the family of quadratic Dirichlet $L$-functions of prime-related moduli under the generalized Riemann hypothesis.

Symplectic duality for the constant term of the geometric Eisenstein series

Igor Chaban

Symplectic duality is a conjectural mirror symmetry between pairs of algebraic spaces arising in physics. This paper studies the cohomology of a geometric object encoding the 'constant term' of the geometric Eisenstein series — a tool in the Langlands program — and reveals a hidden Coulomb branch symmetry (here an A_n surface singularity) acting on it, providing concrete evidence for symplectic duality in a previously inaccessible Langlands-theoretic setting.

math.AG math-ph math.RT
Original abstract

We study the cohomology of a quasimap space that categorifies the constant term of the geometric Eisenstein series for the mirabolic parabolic subgroup of $GL$ over the function field $\mathbb{F}_q(C)$ of a smooth projective curve $C$. This cohomology carries a natural action of an algebra of correspondences whose commutative subalgebra is the ring of regular functions on the Coulomb branch, which here is the $A_{n}$-surface singularity. A choice of rank-one local system on $C$ induces an action of the étale fundamental group on the Coulomb branch; the scheme-theoretic fixed locus carries a natural vector bundle. Our main result identifies the cohomology of the quasimap space with the local cohomology of this vector bundle, for a generic range of parameters.

Quasi-affine schemes and singly compactly generated $t$-structures

Giovanni Rossanigo

When can a scheme (a geometric object in algebraic geometry) be detected as 'quasi-affine' — essentially embeddable in affine space — purely from its derived category of sheaves? This paper shows that quasi-affineness is equivalent to the non-negative part of the derived category being generated by a single compact object. This gives a clean categorical characterization of an important geometric property, connecting abstract homotopy-theoretic tools to classical geometry.

math.AG
Original abstract

We show that for a quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme $X$ with an ample family of line bundles, the connective half $\text{QCoh}(X)_{\geq0}$ of the standard $t$-structure on the derived $\infty$-category of quasi-coherent sheaves is compactly generated by a connective perfect object if and only if $X$ is quasi-affine.

A Symmetry Method for Key Vanishing Lemmas and Optimal $2$-Jet Thresholds for Hyperbolicity

Tao Cui, Lei Hou, Jianjun Liu et al.

A complex manifold is hyperbolic if no non-constant holomorphic map from the complex plane into it exists; proving hyperbolicity often requires 'jet differentials,' which detect obstructions to such maps. This paper introduces a symmetry-based method using an involution on Fermat-type equations to establish key vanishing results for 2-jet differentials, improving bounds toward proving generic hyperbolic surfaces in projective 3-space satisfy optimal geometric thresholds.

math.AG
Original abstract

This manuscript reports on an ongoing project. We develop a new symmetry-based method for establishing vanishing results for negatively twisted invariant 2-jet differentials, both on generic surfaces in $\mathbb{P}^3$ and on complements of generic plane curves. The approach improves upon the recent work of Hou--Huynh--Merker--Xie by using a two-term perturbation of Fermat-type equations that is invariant under an involution exchanging two coordinates. The induced linear action on the space of invariant 2-jet differentials, combined with an elementary eigenvector argument from the representation theory of $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, generates numerous additional linear constraints. This transforms the previously intractable systems into highly overdetermined ones, which we solve with a C++ implementation incorporating parallelization and modular arithmetic. Our ultimate goal is to prove that a very generic surface in $\mathbb{P}^3$ of degree $d \geqslant 15$ is Kobayashi hyperbolic, and that the complement of a generic curve in $\mathbb{P}^2$ of degree $d \geqslant 11$ is hyperbolic. As a concrete step, we prove here that a very generic surface in $\mathbb{P}^3$ of degree $d \geqslant 16$ is Kobayashi hyperbolic. The method developed in this paper also lays the theoretical foundation for the vanishing results needed to reach the optimal bounds. Since many colleagues have asked to see the new symmetry method, we are making this manuscript available now. The C++ code for the remaining cases $d=15$ and $d=11$ is already written and the computations are underway; we expect to complete them in the near future.

Coincidence Correspondences and Nonlinear Root Geometry

Alok Shukla

Root systems and Coxeter groups usually arise from reflection symmetries, but this paper shows they can emerge directly from finite maps between algebraic varieties. By analyzing the self-fiber product of a finite morphism—the variety tracking pairs of points with the same image—the authors extract Cartan-type invariants without any reference to reflections. This suggests root-theoretic structures are more broadly geometric than previously recognized.

math.AG
Original abstract

We show that finite morphisms of smooth algebraic varieties naturally give rise to Cartan--Coxeter type structures. Starting from the self-fiber product $X\times_YX$ of a finite morphism $Q:X\to Y$, we construct local symmetry operators and intrinsic Cartan-type invariants from the geometry of its non-diagonal irreducible components. This provides a mechanism for reconstructing root-theoretic structures directly from algebraic correspondences rather than from reflection groups. A central part of the theory is a rank-two geometry associated with pairs of non-diagonal components. We establish a rank-two reduction theorem, derive explicit trace and determinant formulas for the corresponding operators, and obtain a classification into elliptic, parabolic, and hyperbolic transport types. These results yield intrinsic analogues of Cartan matrices, Coxeter transformations, exponents, and Dynkin diagrams associated with finite morphisms. We further prove rigidity theorems showing that the structures arising from a single finite morphism are highly constrained. To obtain richer geometries, we introduce transport atlases of compatible local finite covers equipped with connection data, leading to nonlinear Cartan fields with variable local geometry. This places classical Weyl and complex reflection geometries within a broader correspondence-based root theory extending beyond finite reflection groups.

A Pascal-type construction of the Segre cubic and the Cremona--Richmond configuration

Piotr Pokora, Tomasz Szemberg

Pascal's theorem says six points on a conic produce collinear intersections; this paper builds an analogous construction one dimension higher. Starting from two quadruples of hyperplanes in four-dimensional projective space satisfying a natural condition, the authors show twelve residual intersection planes always lie on a cubic threefold—typically the classical Segre cubic—recovering a combinatorial structure called the Cremona-Richmond configuration and producing a new geometric object called a (5,3)-geprofi set.

math.AG math.AC
Original abstract

We present a Pascal-type residual construction in P^4. Starting from two quadruples of hyperplanes whose four diagonal intersection planes lie in a hyperplane, we show that the twelve residual planes lie on a cubic threefold. In the general case this cubic is the Segre cubic, and the construction recovers its fifteen planes and the associated Cremona--Richmond configuration. We also exhibit a point-line realization of this configuration in P^4 and show that it gives a (5,3)-geprofi set.

Riccardo Carini

Irreducible symplectic varieties are higher-dimensional analogues of K3 surfaces with exceptionally rich geometry, but constructing new examples with large second Betti numbers is hard. This paper produces a new series in dimensions 4 through 20 by taking quotients of certain moduli spaces on K3 surfaces related to del Pezzo surfaces, achieving second Betti numbers up to 24—expanding the known landscape of these rare, highly symmetric spaces.

math.AG

We construct a series of irreducible symplectic varieties of dimension $2n$, for $2\leq n\leq 10$, with second Betti numbers $16\leq b_2\leq 24$. They arise as non-trivial terminalisations of finite symplectic quotients of Beauville-Mukai systems on very general K3-del Pezzo double covers.

Single-valued polylogarithms for higher genera

Konstantin Baune, Johannes Broedel, Yannis Moeckli

Polylogarithms are classical functions that appear throughout number theory and physics, but on curved surfaces they develop complicated monodromy—they change value when analytically continued around loops. This paper constructs 'single-valued' versions on higher-genus Riemann surfaces (generalizing donut-shaped and more complex topologies), extending previous genus-one work and connecting to tools used in string theory amplitude calculations.

hep-th math-ph math.AG math.NT
Original abstract

We extend the construction of single-valued polylogarithms at genus one from arXiv:2511.15240 to once-punctured Riemann surfaces of higher genera. The resulting functions have a trivial monodromy representation with respect to the fundamental group, hence they descend to well-defined functions on the surface. Very stable parabolic $G$-Higgs bundles and affine flag varieties

Miguel González

Higgs bundles are pairs of a vector bundle and a 'Higgs field,' and 'very stable' ones are those whose upward flow under a natural circle action is closed—a condition controlling the geometry of the moduli space. For parabolic G-Higgs bundles over punctured curves, this paper classifies which fixed points of that action are very stable, expressing the answer through combinatorial data in affine flag varieties associated to the group G.

math.AG math.DG
Original abstract

Let $G$ be a semisimple complex algebraic group. We study the $\mathbb C^\times$-action on the moduli space of strongly parabolic $G$-Higgs bundles over a punctured curve with weights in the interior of the standard Weyl alcove. We describe its fixed points and, for the components with generically regular Higgs field, we classify the fixed points that have closed Bialynicki-Birula upward flows, called very stable. The classification is stated in terms of a naturally associated divisor valued in the extended affine Weyl group of $G$ which encodes the information of the fixed point. Under this point of view, very stable fixed points naturally coincide with divisors where the coefficients are minimal under the Bruhat order. Furthermore, when the divisor is supported at the parabolic punctures, the corresponding very stable upward flow is a section of the Hitchin map. For these upward flows, we propose a conjectural mirror line bundle over the dual moduli space, and verify its validity for $G=\mathrm{PGL}_n(\mathbb{C})$.

The Grothendieck-Katz Conjecture for privileged local systems

Hélène Esnault, Michael Groechenig

The Grothendieck-Katz conjecture predicts that a differential equation with algebraic coefficients has finite monodromy if and only if it has 'p-curvature zero' for almost all primes p. This paper introduces 'privileged' local systems—a higher-dimensional generalization of Katz's rigid local systems—and proves the conjecture holds for them, extending Katz's middle convolution techniques beyond the classical one-dimensional setting.

math.AG math.NT
Original abstract

We define study privileged local systems on a smooth quasi-projective complex variety $X$ with fixed quasi-unipotent monodromies around a boundary divisor. The notion is a generalization of Katz' physical rigidity on an open of $\mathbb P^1$. It is defined in any dimension and includes non-rigid local systems. In the latter case, Katz used the \emph{middle convolution} procedure to classify all physically local systems and derived several consequences such as the $p$-curvature conjecture for local systems of this type. We present a new proof, which avoids the use of middle convolution. This yields examples in higher dimension of local systems which verify the $p$-curvature conjecture.

Blow-Up Constructions and Applications to Segre Classes and Multidegree Formulas

Kai Huang

Blow-ups are a geometric tool for resolving singularities by replacing a troublesome point or subspace with something more manageable. This paper compares two blow-up strategies—simultaneous multigraded versus iterated—showing they're birationally equivalent, then uses this to derive concrete formulas for intersection-theoretic invariants (Chern classes, Segre classes, multidegrees). The payoff includes a proof of the product formula for mixed multiplicities, settling an open problem in commutative algebra.

math.AG
Original abstract

By comparing simultaneous multigraded blow-ups with suitable iterated blow-up construction, we establish a birational correspondence between the associated exceptional divisors. We further investigate blow-ups arising from rational maps to multiprojective spaces and derive intersection-theoretic formulas for the Chern classes of the resulting exceptional divisors and pullback of tautological line bundles. These constructions have two main applications. First, they yield a proof of the product formula for Segre classes without any pure-dimensionality hypothesis. Second, they lead to a degree formula for arbitrary closed subschemes of multiprojective spaces, recovering and extending the classical formula of van der Waerden.

Yenni Cherik

Lipschitz geometry studies spaces by how distances behave metrically, capturing finer information than topology alone. This paper gives a complete classification of complex surface germs (spaces near a singular point) up to inner Lipschitz equivalence—including surfaces with non-isolated singularities, which had resisted prior classification. The complete invariant is expressed in terms of numerical data from the normalization map, extending a landmark earlier result to the remaining hard cases.

math.AG
Original abstract

Let $(X,0)$ be a germ of a reduced and irreducible complex surface embedded in $(\mathbb{C}^k,0)$. In this paper, we give a complete invariant of the inner Lipschitz geometry of complex surface germs, extending the result of Birbrair--Neumann--Pichon \cite{BNP} to the non-isolated case. Minimizers for Coulomb gases constrained to a halfspace

Rupert L. Frank, Paata Ivanishvili, Clara Torres-Latorre

When charged particles interact via Coulomb forces in a quadratic trap but are restricted to a halfspace, how does their equilibrium distribution change as that boundary moves? This paper proves a phase transition exists in this constrained mean-field model, confirming a recent conjecture by Byun, Forrester, Majumdar, and Schehr. The result clarifies how geometric constraints cause qualitative, abrupt changes in particle distributions rather than smooth continuous shifts.

math.AP math-ph
Original abstract

We consider a family of optimization problems, based on a mean-field description of particles interacting through Coulomb forces in a quadratic trap. In addition, the particles are constrained to lie in a halfspace and we are interested in the way the particle distribution changes as the halfspace varies. In particular, we can prove the existence of a phase transition, thereby settling a recent conjecture by Byun, Forrester, Majumdar and Schehr.

Scattering for the 4D Zakharov system below the ground state

Timothy Candy, Kenji Nakanishi

The Zakharov system models the interaction of plasma waves and is known to develop singularities for large initial data; the 'ground state' is the critical solution separating safe from dangerous regimes. This paper proves that in four dimensions, any solution starting inside the 'potential well' — a region of energy space below the ground state — exists for all time and scatters (disperses like a free wave), completing a program started in prior work by ruling out pathological compact solutions.

math.AP
Original abstract

For the Zakharov system in four space dimensions, we prove that all solutions inside the potential well of the ground states are global and scattering in the energy space, with no other restriction such as symmetry. The proof has already been reduced by [3] to ruling out the existence of a minimal non-scattering solution that is precompact along some trajectory. This paper carries out the final step in the proof, namely we exclude the possibility of precompact solutions inside the potential well by combining two distinct arguments depending on the motion of trajectory.

Norm inflation for the cubic hyperbolic NLS on $\mathbb T^2$

Shunlin Shen, Yuzhao Wang

The hyperbolic nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) replaces the usual Laplacian with an indefinite operator, making solutions behave more wildly. This paper proves 'norm inflation' — solutions starting arbitrarily close to zero in Sobolev spaces H^s can instantly become large — for nearly all s ≤ 1/2, showing the equation is severely ill-posed. Remarkably, the instability mechanisms above and below the critical regularity s=0 are completely different, with the hyperbolic geometry driving a new phenomenon in the subcritical regime.

math.AP
Original abstract

We prove norm inflation for the cubic hyperbolic nonlinear Schrödinger equation in $H^s(\mathbb T^2)$ for every $s\in(-\infty,0)\cup(0,\frac12]$. The scaling-critical point $s=0$ is excluded by conservation of the $L^2$ norm. The strong ill-posedness below and above the scaling-critical point arises from two completely different mechanisms. Particularly in the scaling-subcritical regime, this dynamical instability stems from the hyperbolic nature. Together with the local well-posedness result in \cite{WangHNLS}, this gives a sharp dichotomy away from the mass space $L^2(\mathbb T^2)$: local well-posedness holds for $s>\frac12$, whereas norm inflation occurs for all $s\le \frac12$ with $s\ne0$.

Delayed blow-up by transport noise for the 3D Navier-Stokes equation with Navier-slip boundary conditions

Meng Zhao

Whether 3D Navier-Stokes equations can spontaneously blow up (develop infinite velocities) is one of mathematics' great open problems. This paper shows that adding transport noise—random stirring terms—to the vorticity formulation can delay any potential blow-up past any prescribed time T with high probability. This is among the first such results with physically realistic boundary conditions, suggesting noise can regularize fluid equations.

math.AP math.PR
Original abstract

We study the vorticity formulation of the 3D Navier-Stokes equation driven by transport noise in a periodic channel with Navier-slip boundary conditions. We consider both non-degenerate transport noise and degenerate tangential transport noise. For any prescribed $T>0$ and $ε>0$, we prove that, by choosing the noise intensity sufficiently large and concentrating the noise on sufficiently high modes, the solution exists up to $T$ with probability at least $1-ε$. A main contribution of this work is to identify and analyze the interaction between enhanced dissipation induced by transport noise and physical boundary effects. The no-flux condition breaks the isotropy of the noise and changes the scaling limit of the Itô-Stratonovich corrector. In the non-degenerate case, a boundary feedback term appears in the limiting effective operator; in the degenerate case, the limiting operator is a nonlocal anisotropic tangential dissipation.
