Math Research Digest

Welcome to this week's edition of Grounded, a particularly rich issue spanning fluid dynamics, combinatorics, operator algebras, and arithmetic geometry. Two themes stand out immediately for their audacity. First, the study of fluid singularities is advancing on multiple fronts simultaneously: one paper delivers a computer-assisted proof technique for 3D Euler blowup by analytically enforcing the delicate vanishing conditions that numerical approximations miss, while another shows—strikingly—that adding physically motivated transport noise to the 3D Navier-Stokes equations prevents blowup with high probability for *arbitrarily large* initial data, suggesting that stochasticity can act as a genuine regularizing mechanism rather than a mere perturbation. Second, Ramsey theory is having an exceptional week: canonical Ramsey results are extended to all products of simplices and to all nondegenerate simplices in the infinite-coloring setting, the exact growth rate of rainbow arithmetic progression thresholds is pinned down as Θ(k² log k) rather than the conjectured k², and the Hales-Jewett upper bounds are tightened from Wainer-hierarchy towers to merely double-exponential ones. Elsewhere, a 35-year-old conjecture from statistical physics meets its end: the storage capacity of the Ising perceptron is confirmed to nine decimal places

A Bombieri-Vinogradov theorem for exponential sums over products of k primes

Pierre-Alexandre Bazin

How large can partial sums of a number-theoretic exponential sum get? This paper proves a Bombieri-Vinogradov theorem—a result averaging error terms in prime distribution over arithmetic progressions—for sums over integers with exactly k prime factors (counted with multiplicity). The payoff is a power-saving lower bound on the maximum of these oscillating sums, showing they can't stay suspiciously small, which advances understanding of the pseudorandomness of almost-primes.

math.NT
Original abstract

We prove a Bombieri-Vinogradov type theorem for exponential sums over products of $k$ primes. As an application, we show the lower bound $$\sup_{n\le x} \left|\sum_{m\le n} 1_{Ω(m) = k} e(αm)\right| \gg x^{1/6 - \varepsilon}$$ for $2\le k\le (2-\varepsilon)\log\log x$ and $α\in\mathbb{R},$ where we noted $e(β) := e^{2iπβ}.$

On a character-twisted analogue of Schäffer's equation

Kálmán Györy, Vandita Patel, Ákos Pintér et al.

This paper studies a twisted variant of a classical Diophantine equation, where the terms are weighted by a quadratic character (a number-theoretic function encoding symmetry mod f). For most valid moduli m, the equation has no integer solutions with exponent n ≥ 2. The result extends powerful methods from the theory of Bernoulli polynomials to rule out perfect-power sums in a broad new setting.

math.NT
Original abstract

Let $f$ be a positive integer, and let $χ$ be a primitive quadratic character of conductor $f$. Let $k$ be a positive integer, and write $B_k(χ,X)$ for the $k$-th Bernoulli polynomial corresponding to $χ$. Suppose $B_k(χ,X)$ is irreducible and of degree at least $2$. Then for 100% of positive integers $m$ divisible by $f$, the Diophantine equation \[ χ(1) \cdot (x+1)^k+χ(2) \cdot (x+2)^k+\cdots+χ(m) \cdot (x+m)^k \, =\, y^n, \] has no solutions with $x$, $y$, $n$ integers, and $n \ge 2$.

Equidistribution and the torsor method

Christian Bernert, Ulrich Derenthal, Florian Wilsch

Manin's conjecture predicts how rational points distribute on Fano varieties as height (a measure of arithmetic complexity) grows. This paper proves the conjecture for smooth quintic del Pezzo surfaces over number fields, establishing that rational points equidistribute with respect to any anticanonical height. The authors develop a general framework for the torsor method that should be broadly applicable to related counting problems.

math.NT math.AG
Original abstract

We prove equidistribution and Manin's conjecture for rational points outside the lines on smooth split quintic del Pezzo surfaces over number fields with respect to any anticanonical height. The proof is based on a general theorem that is broadly usable to deduce equidistribution when using the torsor method with several equivalent height functions to treat variants of Manin's problem.

The First Moment of Dirichlet L-functions over Generators of the Character Group

Rizwanur Khan

Dirichlet L-functions are number-theoretic functions encoding information about prime distributions in arithmetic progressions, and their central values L(1/2) relate to deep conjectures. This paper computes the average of L(1/2, χ) over generators of the character group modulo a prime q, a finer averaging than the classical mean over all characters. The result refines our understanding of how L-function values distribute.

math.NT
Original abstract

We evaluate the average of the central values $L(1/2,χ)$ over the generators of the group of Dirichlet characters modulo $q$, as $q\to\infty$ through the primes.

Strongly complete sets and a conjecture of Erdős

Steve Fan

A set of natural numbers is 'complete' if every sufficiently large integer is a sum of distinct elements, and 'strongly complete' if it stays complete even after removing finitely many elements. This paper proves a sufficient condition for strong completeness involving both a density requirement and a Diophantine equidistribution condition, and uses it to resolve a conjecture of Erdős about when completeness is robust to finite perturbations.

math.NT math.CO
Original abstract

A set $A\subseteq\mathbb{N}$ is called $\textit{complete}$ if every sufficiently large integer can be written as a sum of distinct elements of $A$. It is $\textit{strongly complete}$ if it remains complete after one deletes finitely many elements from it. We show that $A\subseteq\mathbb{N}$ is strongly complete whenever \[ \big|A\cap(2^k,2^{k+1}]\big|\ge6 \] for every sufficiently large $k\in\mathbb{N}$, and \[ \sum_{a\in A}\|aθ\|=\infty, \quad\forallθ\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z}. \] In particular, this resolves a 1961 conjecture of Erdős. The proof builds on previous work of Bergelson and Simmons. Our approach also allows us to establish a more general strong-completeness criterion with suitable ordered blocks in place of dyadic intervals.

Exact classification of elliptic curves $y^{2}=x^{3}-pqx$ with rank $0$ and trivial $\Sha[2]$

Arkabrata Ghosh, Paul M. Voutier

Which elliptic curves of the form y²=x³−pqx have the simplest possible arithmetic? This paper gives exact conditions on the primes p and q that force the curve to have rank zero (no infinite-order rational points) and trivial 2-torsion in its Shafarevich-Tate group (a measure of arithmetic obstruction). The result matters because fully classifying curves with rank 0 and no hidden obstructions is rare, and the conditions here are both necessary and sufficient.

math.NT
Original abstract

For the elliptic curves $E_{p,q}: y^{2}=x^{3}-pqx$ where $p$ and $q$ are distinct odd primes, we establish necessary and sufficient conditions under which rank$\,E_{p,q}(\mathbb{Q})$ and $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{2}} \Sha \left( E_{p,q}/\bbQ \right)[2]$ are both $0$. We do so via a similar characterisation of when the Selmer groups associated with the degree-$2$ isogeny $φ$ and its dual $\widehatφ$ are both of minimal size, along with results about a cokernel that arises from a related exact sequence.

Area correlations related to lattice points in discs

Matteo Bordignon, Pär Kurlberg

This paper investigates how lattice points (integer coordinate points) distribute near the boundary of expanding circles, motivated by a conjecture of Lester and Wigman that certain area correlations vanish. By connecting the problem to circle flows on tori — related to charged particle motion in magnetic fields — the authors prove an analogue of the conjecture in this dynamical setting, establishing a surprising mixing property despite the flow being essentially integrable.

math.NT
Original abstract

Motivated by the Lester-Wigman vanishing area correlation conjecture for lattice points near the boundary of circles of growing radius, we investigate the dynamics of circle flows on tori (which are related to the motion of a charged particle in a magnetic field on a torus.) We show that an analogue of the vanishing correlation conjecture holds in this setting, i.e., we have "mixing for the area observable" despite the flow being essentially integrable. We also determine the probability density function of the areas, in global as well as local regimes.

Bernoulli determinants and cuspidal subgroups

François Brunault

The cuspidal class group of a modular curve measures arithmetic complexity at the curve's 'cusps' (boundary points), and computing its order is a classical number-theory problem. This paper gives an explicit formula for this order for the modular curve X₁(N) at every integer N, derived by evaluating a determinant built from Bernoulli polynomials. The authors also propose a higher-weight generalization, suggesting a deeper pattern connecting these determinants to algebraic invariants of modular curves.

math.NT
Original abstract

We give an explicit formula for the order of the rational cuspidal class group of the modular curve $X_1(N)$ for an arbitrary integer $N$. The proof relies on results of Streng on the group of modular units on $X_1(N)$, and requires computing a certain determinant involving the second Bernoulli polynomial. We also define a higher weight analogue of the cuspidal class group and speculate that its order is related to a similar determinant defined using a higher degree Bernoulli polynomial.

Spectral fourth moments of Hecke--Maaß cusp forms

Edgar Assing, Peter Humphries

How large can automorphic forms get at special points? This paper establishes near-optimal bounds on the fourth moment of Hecke–Maaß cusp forms (eigenfunctions of certain differential operators on hyperbolic surfaces) evaluated at Heegner points (special algebraically significant points). The results sharpen our understanding of how these forms concentrate and yield new progress on counting problems involving sums of two squares.

math.NT
Original abstract

In this note, we establish essentially optimal bounds for certain spectral moments of automorphic forms for $\mathrm{GL}(2)$. More precisely, we consider the family of Hecke--Maaß cusp forms with spectral parameter in a dyadic interval and study the fourth moment of these forms evaluated at a Heegner point. We additionally present applications of our main result to the shifted convolution problem involving the sum of two squares function $r(n)$ as well as to pointwise Diophantine exponents.

On the Second Moment of $L (1/2, \mathrm{As} (f))$

Changlin Li, Zhi Qi

Asai L-functions are analytic objects encoding arithmetic data from Hilbert modular forms over real quadratic fields. This paper proves an asymptotic formula for the average of the square of these L-functions at the central point as the weight grows, with a power-saving error term. Such moment formulas are key tools for detecting nonvanishing of L-values and understanding their distribution.

math.NT
Original abstract

Let $\mathbf{F}$ be a real quadratic field. Let $f $ traverse a Hecke orthonormal basis of Hilbert cusp forms over $ \mathbf{F} $ of full level and parallel weight $(k,k)$. As $k \rightarrow \infty$, we prove an asymptotic formula for the second moment of central Asai $L$-values $L (1/2, \mathrm{As} (f))$: \begin{equation*} {\sum}_{f } \, ω_f L(1/2,\mathrm{As}(f))^2 = P_3 ( \log {k } ) k^2 + O_{\mathbf{F},\varepsilon} (k^{3/2 + \varepsilon} ), \end{equation*} where $ω_f$ are the harmonic weights and $P_3 (X)$ is an explicit polynomial of degree $3$. This refines the mean Lindelöf bound $ O_{\mathbf{F},\varepsilon} (k^{2 + \varepsilon} ) $ proved by Wenzhi Luo.

Ranks of Elliptic Curves Twisted by Quadratic Forms

Mohammad H. Hamdar, Cihan Sabuncu

An elliptic curve's rank measures how many independent rational points it has — a central mystery in number theory. This paper proves that for any elliptic curve over the rationals, infinitely many of its quadratic twists (rescaled versions) by numbers expressible as sums of two squares have rank exactly 1. The result refines earlier work by capturing contributions from lower-order derivatives of L-functions that previous methods missed.

math.NT
Original abstract

Let $E$ be an elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ and let $E^d$ be its twist by the quadratic character $χ_d$. We prove there are infinitely many twists $d$ which are sums of two squares such that $E^d$ has rank $1$. This result is achieved using moments of derivatives of modular $L$-functions, and particularly captures the lower derivatives which were left out in the work of Munshi. Such a result, in particular, also gives us information on the elliptic fibration $(1+t^2)y^2=f(x)$, where $f(x)$ is a cubic polynomial.

Euler systems and the symmetric square of a Hida family

Debanjana Kundu, Jishnu Ray, Stefano Vigni

This paper constructs an Euler system—a powerful algebraic tool used to control arithmetic objects called Selmer groups—for the symmetric square of a p-adic family of modular forms called a Hida family. The authors also prove a functional equation relating a Selmer group to its dual. These results push the Iwasawa–Greenberg Main Conjecture program, which links analytic p-adic L-functions to algebraic Selmer groups, into a broader interpolated family setting.

math.NT math.AG
Original abstract

Let $p\geq7$ be a prime number. We build a non-trivial Euler system for the symmetric square of a $p$-adic Hida family of modular forms interpolating the Euler system constructed by Loeffler-Zerbes for the symmetric square of a $p$-ordinary newform. As a second contribution, we prove an algebraic functional equation for dual Selmer groups in this setting. Finally, building on recent work by Büyükboduk-Ganguly on functional equations of algebraic (Rankin-Selberg) $p$-adic $L$-functions, we prove a divisibility result towards the Iwasawa main conjecture for the symmetric square of a Hida family.

Adjoint Bloch--Kato Selmer groups of regular algebraic automorphic Galois representations

Lambert A'Campo, Bence Hevesi, Jack A. Thorne et al.

Bloch–Kato Selmer groups are arithmetic objects that measure how a Galois representation 'varies' p-adically, and their vanishing is crucial for proving uniqueness results in the Langlands program. This paper shows the adjoint Selmer group vanishes for Galois representations attached to a broad class of automorphic representations of GL_n over CM fields (fields with a complex conjugation symmetry). The key advance is avoiding assumptions on residual representations mod p, which had blocked previous approaches.

math.NT
Original abstract

We prove the vanishing of the adjoint Bloch--Kato Selmer group of the Galois representations associated to regular algebraic automorphic representations of general linear groups over CM fields. A key novelty of our work is that we impose conditions only on the $p$-adic Galois representations, and not on their associated residual representations modulo $p$.

Local-global compatibility of automorphic Galois representations over CM fields at $p$

Lambert A'Campo, Bence Hevesi, Jack A. Thorne et al.

To every nice automorphic representation of GL_n over a CM field, number theorists attach a family of p-adic Galois representations, but confirming these match local arithmetic data at p has been a major technical challenge. This paper proves these representations are potentially semi-stable — a precise p-adic Hodge theory condition — and compatible with the local Langlands correspondence (the dictionary between local Galois theory and representation theory) up to semi-simplification. This closes a significant gap in the local-global compatibility program for automorphic Galois representations.

math.NT
Original abstract

Let $F$ be a CM number field; then, to any cuspidal, regular algebraic automorphic representation of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbf{A}_F)$ is associated a compatible system of $p$-adic Galois representations of the absolute Galois group of $F$. We prove that these representations are potentially semi-stable, in the sense of $p$-adic Hodge theory, and satisfy compatibility with the local Langlands correspondence, up to semi-simplification.

The rank of the modular multiplication matrix

Haojia Shi

The modular multiplication matrix encodes how multiplication by integers works modulo n, and its rank—the dimension of its row space—captures hidden arithmetic structure. This paper proves a conjectural rank formula for every positive integer by combining additive Fourier analysis with multiplicative character theory on unit groups, closing a conjecture of Bueno et al. and revealing a clean interplay between additive and multiplicative structure in modular arithmetic.

math.NT
Original abstract

We prove Bueno et al.'s conjectural formula for the rank of the modular multiplication matrix for every positive integer. The proof first applies the additive Fourier transform to split the row space by exact additive character order, and then applies the multiplicative character decomposition on unit groups to count the nonzero components. The same formula holds after deleting the zero row and zero column.

Orbit Transversality for Abelian Varieties

Kaiwen Lu

Given an abelian variety (a higher-dimensional generalization of an elliptic curve) over a number field and a self-map on it, how spread out are its orbits? This paper proves that any collection of representative points—one from each 'grand orbit' (the full two-sided trajectory of a point)—is always Zariski dense, meaning it can't be confined to any proper algebraic subvariety. This confirms a strong dynamical property for a broad and important class of algebraic objects.

math.NT math.AG math.DS
Original abstract

Let $X/K$ be an abelian variety defined over a number field and let $f:X\to X$ be a dominant morphism defined over $K$. We show that $(f,K)$ is strongly dense orbit transversal. That is, up to replacing $K$ with a finite extension, every set of representatives for grand $(f,K)$-orbits is Zariski dense in $X$.

On the geometry of Coulomb branches

Ben Webster

Coulomb branches are geometric spaces arising from 3D quantum field theories, carrying a rich 'symplectic singularity' structure — meaning they locally resemble the singularities of symplectic quotients. The authors give a uniform geometric description via blowups of toric compactifications across three settings (rational, K-theoretic, elliptic), classifying their symplectic resolutions and settling which Coulomb branches admit the smooth replacements essential for geometric representation theory.

math.AG math-ph
Original abstract

Coulomb branches of 3-dimensional $N=4$ gauge theories, as defined by Braverman--Finkelberg--Nakajima, form a large and interesting class of symplectic singularities. In this paper, we give a geometric description of these varieties in terms of blowups of toric compactifications, uniformly across the rational, $K$-theoretic, and elliptic settings, which in physics are often associated with 3-, 4-, and 5-dimensional versions of these theories. We use the resulting local description to classify the symplectic leaves of Coulomb branches and describe their transverse slices in terms of the classification of zero-dimensional leaves of certain smaller Coulomb branches. We also discuss related topics, including removing an unnecessary hypothesis from a theorem of Gannon and the author on functoriality for Coulomb branches, and clarifying the relationship with transverse Hilbert schemes by refining the approach of Bielawski and Foscolo.

Zeros of one-forms and the topology of algebraic maps

Stefan Schreieder, Botong Wang

Can a map between complex algebraic varieties behave like a fiber bundle topologically but fail to do so geometrically? This paper constructs a projective variety where the Albanese map (a canonical map to a complex torus) is a homotopy fiber bundle yet not a smooth submersion, and where every holomorphic one-form vanishes somewhere. This separates topological and analytic notions of fibration, resolving subtle questions about the relationship between one-forms and the topology of algebraic maps.

math.AG math.CV
Original abstract

We construct a smooth complex projective variety whose Albanese morphism is a homotopy fiber bundle but not a submersion. The same variety fibers smoothly over the circle, although every holomorphic one-form on it has a zero. A second construction yields smooth complex projective varieties $X$ such that the Aomoto complex of every nonzero holomorphic one-form on every connected finite étale cover of $X$ is exact, while $X$ admits no real closed one-form without zeros. The two constructions build, respectively, on a homology fiber bundle of Corrêa--Kollár that is not a homotopy fiber bundle and on a rational cohomology torus constructed by Debarre--Jiang--Lahoz. Consequently, we disprove Kotschick's conjecture, the remaining implication in the Bobadilla--Kollár conjecture, and a conjecture of the first-named author.

Formality for rigid-analytic spaces satisfying the weight-monodromy conjecture

Alexander Petrov, Bogdan Zavyalov

Formality means a topological space's algebraic invariants are determined entirely by cohomology, with no higher-order 'Massey product' information surviving. This paper proves that smooth proper rigid-analytic spaces — geometric objects over p-adic fields — have formal cohomology algebras whenever they satisfy the weight-monodromy conjecture, a major open conjecture in arithmetic geometry. Counterexamples without that hypothesis show the condition is genuinely necessary.

math.AG math.NT
Original abstract

We prove that étale and de Rham cohomology algebras of a smooth proper rigid-analytic space over a finite extension of $\mathbf{Q}_p$ are formal if the rigid-analytic space satisfies the weight-monodromy conjecture. We give examples of smooth proper rigid-analytic surfaces whose cohomology algebras are not formal.

Topological reconstruction theorems over uncountable algebraically closed fields

Benjamin Castle, Ronan O'Gorman

Can you recover an algebraic variety—a geometric object defined by polynomial equations—just from knowing which sets are open in its topology? Over uncountable algebraically closed fields, this paper extends recent reconstruction theorems to all quasi-projective varieties in any characteristic, answering open questions posed by the original authors. The key innovation is importing model-theoretic tools (logic-based techniques for studying mathematical structures) to handle cases previously out of reach.

math.AG
Original abstract

Working over uncountable algebraically closed fields, we extend the theorems of Kollár-Lieblich-Olsson-Sawin on reconstructing varieties from their Zariski topological spaces. In particular, we adapt their results to arbitrary quasi-projective varieties in arbitrary characteristic, and thus we give positive answers to each of the relevant `speculations' made by the original authors in our setting. Our proofs use techniques from model theory: in particular, we employ a general model-theoretic setting for algebro-geometric reconstruction problems, known as the `Zilber trichotomy for ACF-relics'.

On the Feyzbakhsh-Thomas programme for Fano $3$-folds

Ivan Karpov, Miguel Moreira

How are Donaldson-Thomas invariants—counts of geometric objects called sheaves on a 3D space—related across different ranks? For Fano 3-folds (positively curved algebraic varieties) satisfying a curvature inequality, the paper reduces rank-r sheaf counts to rank-0 counts, extending a programme previously only known for Calabi-Yau varieties. This gives a powerful new tool for computing these invariants, which connect geometry, physics, and string theory.

math.AG
Original abstract

Let $X$ be a Fano $3$-fold with even canonical class which satisfies the generalized Bogomolov-Gieseker inequality, such as $\mathbb P^3$. We express Donaldson-Thomas invariants counting Gieseker semistable sheaves of rank $r$, where $r > 0$, on $X$ in terms of those counting sheaves of rank $0$ and pure dimension $2$. This implements an analogue of the programme initiated by S. Feyzbakhsh and R. Thomas in the case of Calabi-Yau varieties. The methods include $K$-theoretic Donaldson-Thomas theory and, quite unexpectedly, the use of certain combinatorial properties of vertex algebras.

Non-cancellative varieties, maximal tori, and the Makar-Limanov invariant

Sergey Gaifullin, Mikhail Petrov

The Zariski cancellation problem asks whether X×ℂ ≅ Y×ℂ implies X ≅ Y — a higher-dimensional analog of cancellation in algebra that fails in general. This paper constructs a broad new family of counterexamples and shows their cylinders have infinitely many structurally distinct maximal tori (maximal abelian subgroups acting by coordinate rescaling) in their automorphism groups. The work also proves these varieties are 'generically flexible,' meaning their automorphism group acts transitively on a dense open set.

math.AG
Original abstract

In this paper, we construct a wide class of new counterexamples to the generalized Zariski cancellation problem. The cylinders over these counterexamples have infinitely many non-conjugate maximal tori in their regular automorphism groups. We provide an example of a variety with maximal tori of different dimensions in its automorphism group. Additionally, we prove that a cylinder over a non-rigid trinomial variety without a line factor is generically flexible, and hence, has a trivial Makar-Limanov invariant.

A complete curve of genus 105 in the moduli space of curves of genus 3

Christophe Ritzenthaler

The moduli space of genus-3 curves parametrizes all smooth curves of that genus, but finding complete curves (compact, without boundary) inside it is surprisingly hard. This paper constructs such a complete curve of genus 105 living inside that moduli space, contributing to the longstanding question of which moduli spaces of curves contain complete curves.

math.AG
Original abstract

We show the existence of a complete curve of genus 105 in the moduli space of smooth projective curves of genus 3 over the complex field.

Computing Cox rings via the cone conjecture

Tomoki Oda, José Ignacio Yáñez, Juan Pablo Zúñiga

The Cox ring packages all line bundles on an algebraic variety into one algebraic object, and for Calabi-Yau varieties — central objects in string theory and mirror symmetry — computing it is largely open. This paper introduces Morrison-Kawamata dream spaces, a Calabi-Yau analogue of Mori dream spaces, and shows their Cox rings are finitely generated and governed by the same GIT quotient machinery, connecting the cone conjecture in birational geometry to explicit ring computations.

math.AG
Original abstract

We initiate a program to study the Cox ring of Calabi-Yau varieties, employing the notion of Morrison-Kawamata dream spaces. In this setting, we establish an analogue of the Hu-Keel GIT constructions for Mori dream spaces. More precisely, for a Morrison-Kawamata dream space $X$, we establish a correspondence between the small $\mathbb{Q}$-factorial modifications of $X$ and the GIT quotients of $\operatorname{Spec}\operatorname{Cox}(X)$. We further show that the Cox ring of a Morrison-Kawamata dream space is a filtered direct limit of subalgebras, each of which is an inverse limit of finitely generated $\mathrm{Cl}(X)$-graded $\mathbb{K}$-algebras. As an application, we give an explicit presentation of the Cox ring of a very general hypersurface of multidegree $(2,\dots,2,n+1)$ in $(\mathbb{P}^1)^m\times \mathbb{P}^n$. Furthermore, we prove that the Cox ring of such a hypersurface is of dense $F$-pure type.

Fano 4-fold quiver moduli from subspace quivers

Pieter Belmans, Markus Reineke

Quiver moduli spaces are geometric objects built by encoding linear-algebra data (vector spaces and maps between them) into a directed graph called a quiver. This paper fully classifies which moduli spaces arising from 'subspace quivers' are Fano fourfolds—meaning compact complex 4-dimensional spaces whose geometry curves positively inward—finding exactly four, each with rich but tractable structure including finite symmetry groups.

math.AG math.RT
Original abstract

We classify the moduli spaces of representations of subspace quivers which are Fano fourfolds, under a natural assumption on the dimension vector. These moduli spaces can also be described as GIT quotients of products of Grassmannians by the diagonal action of a projective linear group, and there are exactly four of them. They are rational, of pure Hodge-Tate type, infinitesimally rigid, and have finite automorphism groups, with Picard ranks 5, 6, 6 and 7, making them interesting examples in the classification of Fano fourfolds of large Picard rank, as they are not toric or products. Two are known varieties: Manivel's Segre cousin of the Segre cubic 3-fold, and the Fano model of the blowup of $\mathbb{P}^4$ in six points. The other two appear to be new: one is an involution surface bundle over $\mathbb{P}^2$, and the other is a "Segre cousin once-removed", whose geometry closely parallels that of the Segre cousin. Using techniques from quiver moduli, which we survey, we describe the geometry of all four fourfolds in detail.

Metrics on triangulated categories and their enhancements

Alberto Canonaco, Amnon Neeman, Paolo Stellari

Triangulated categories are algebraic structures central to modern geometry, but they often admit multiple 'enhancements' — richer structures encoding extra information. This paper uses a new metric-based framework, lifted to the setting of ∞-categories, to prove that these enhancements are essentially unique across a broad class of triangulated categories, resolving several open questions. The result unifies and greatly extends previously scattered uniqueness theorems in both linear and nonlinear settings.

math.AG math.CT
Original abstract

In this paper we investigate the uniqueness of enhancements of the natural subcategories of weakly approximable triangulated categories. The main idea is to enhance at the level of $\infty$-categories the recently developed theory of excellent metrics. The applications of our results include a vast generalization of the known results about the (strong) uniqueness of enhancements in the linear and nonlinear setting, providing positive answers to some open questions. In addition we prove that, under some natural assumptions, the equivalences between such subcategories can be lifted through their natural inclusions. This completes the picture started in our previous paper arXiv:2505.10374 and extends the known results about Margolis Uniqueness Conjecture for the homotopy category of spectra.

Infinitesimal invariants of genus 4 curves and the Griffiths-Pirola cycle

Matteo Verni

Genus 4 curves are algebraic curves of a specific topological complexity, and the Griffiths-Pirola cycle is a geometric object that encodes subtle algebraic information about families of such curves. This paper gives explicit algebraic formulas for 'infinitesimal invariants' of this cycle, sharpening a classical theorem of Griffiths and providing a new genus-4-specific proof of a nonvanishing result due to Green and Griffiths. The concreteness of the description makes previously abstract invariants directly computable.

math.AG
Original abstract

We give an explicit algebraic description for the infinitesimal invariants associated to the Griffiths-Pirola cycle on the universal genus 4 curve and its self intersection. We apply this to refine a result of Griffiths which recovers from these invariants the equations for the general genus 4 curve, and give a new proof (only in genus 4) of a nonvanishing result of Green and Griffiths.

Isomorphism classes of multi Danielewski varieties

Polina Evdokimova

Danielewski varieties are algebraic surfaces that provide classic examples of non-isomorphic varieties sharing many invariants; this paper studies their higher-dimensional generalizations called multi Danielewski varieties. The authors compute the Makar-Limanov invariant—an algebraic invariant tracking certain symmetries—and fully classify these varieties up to isomorphism. This resolves the isomorphism problem for this family of affine algebraic varieties.

math.AG
Original abstract

We study multi Danielewski varieties, which are the generalization of Danielewski varieties and Double Danielewski varieties. The paper contains description of the Makar-Limanov invariant of these varieties and of their isomorphism classes.

On the invariance of irregular Hodge numbers under crepant birational equivalences

Yichen Qin

The Batyrev–Kontsevich theorem says birationally equivalent Calabi–Yau varieties share Hodge numbers; this paper proves an analogous result for Landau–Ginzburg models, pairs (U, f) of a variety with a regular function. Specifically, irregular Hodge numbers—invariants of the twisted de Rham cohomology capturing the model's singularity structure—are preserved under crepant birational equivalences, which are volume-preserving birational maps. This extends mirror symmetry-motivated invariance results to a broader non-compact setting.

math.AG
Original abstract

The Batyrev--Kontsevich theorem asserts that birational Calabi--Yau varieties have the same Hodge numbers. In this article, we prove an analogue for Landau--Ginzburg models $(U,f)$, consisting of smooth quasi-projective complex varieties $U$ with regular functions $f$ on $U$. We show that the irregular Hodge numbers of the twisted de Rham cohomology $\mathrm{H}^k_{\mathrm{dR}}(U,f)$ are invariant under crepant birational equivalences.

Meromorphic Group Actions and the Support Theorem for Lagrangian Fibrations

Mark Andrea de Cataldo, Yoon-Joo Kim, Christian Schnell
