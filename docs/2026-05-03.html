Frobenius Traces for Rank-2 Drinfeld Modules, Higher-Dimensional Galois Representations, and a Strong Multiplicity One Theorem in Positive Characteristic

Chien-Hua Chen

Two Galois representations—algebraic structures encoding symmetries of number-theoretic objects called Drinfeld modules (function-field analogues of elliptic curves)—are isomorphic if their 'Frobenius traces' (local arithmetic fingerprints) agree almost everywhere. This confirms a positive-characteristic analogue of a classical multiplicity-one theorem. The result clarifies exactly when matching local data forces global isomorphism, with a surprising dimensional threshold governing the general case.

math.NT
Original abstract

In this paper, we prove that if the Frobenius traces agree at all but finitely many places, then two $l$-adic Galois representations, associated to rank-$2$ non-CM Drinfeld modules of generic characteristic, are isomorphic. As a generalization, we show that under a mild dimension hypothesis, the "Frobenius trace equality at all but finitely many places forces isomorphism" between two Galois representations over a local field of positive characteristic only holds under an absolute irreducibility assumption. Moreover, we formulate and prove a function field analogue of strong multiplicity one property for semisimple Galois representations over a local field of positive characteristic.

An improved non-linear Roth-type theorem in finite fields

Mark Lewko

Can every large enough subset of a finite field contain a quadratic progression—three points of the form (x, x+y, x+y²)? This paper proves yes, whenever the subset has at least C|F|^(5/6) elements, improving the previous threshold of |F|^(7/8) using elementary one-variable exponential sum estimates rather than deep multivariate machinery. The result advances the finite-field analogue of Roth's theorem, which asks how dense a set must be to guarantee structured patterns.

math.NT math.CO
Original abstract

Let $F$ be a finite field of odd characteristic. We prove that any set $A\subset F$ with $|A|\geq C|F|^{5/6}$ contains a nontrivial quadratic progression $(x, x+y, x+y^2), y\neq 0.$ For prime fields, this improves the previous best-known exponent of $7/8$, due to Kavrut and Wu. Unlike some of the previous papers, which rely on Katz's deep multivariate exponential-sum estimates, our argument uses only one-variable Weil-type estimates. We also construct, over certain non-prime finite fields, progression-free sets of size $c|F|^{2/3}$. A key idea in the proof was suggested to the author by ChatGPT 5.5.

On the difference between perfect powers and integral $S$-units

Yann Bugeaud

How close can a perfect power z^d get to a smooth number—an integer built only from a fixed set of primes? This paper establishes effective lower bounds showing that the gap |z^d − q₁^a₁···qₜ^aₜ| grows without bound as z^d grows, and similarly for the largest prime factor of that gap. These results refine our understanding of the deep tension between multiplicative and additive structure in the integers.

math.NT
Original abstract

Let $q_1, \ldots , q_t$ be distinct prime numbers. Let $a_1, \ldots , a_t$ be nonnegative integers. We establish effective lower bounds for $|z^d - q_1^{a_1} \ldots q_t^{a_t}|$ and for its greatest prime factor, which tend to infinity with $z^d$, where $z$ is a positive integer coprime with $q_1 \ldots q_t$ and $d \ge 2$ is an integer.

Simple Lie Groups of type An as Galois groups over Q

Stepan Nesterov

The inverse Galois problem asks which groups can appear as symmetry groups of polynomial equations over ℚ. This paper realizes infinite families of projective special linear and unitary groups PSL(n,q) and PSU(n,q) as Galois groups over ℚ—crucially, the first explicit infinite series where the underlying field has arbitrarily large degree over its prime field and the group strictly differs from PGL(n,q) or PGU(n,q).

math.NT
Original abstract

In this paper, we utilize our previous results on mod p monodromy of cyclic coverings of the projective line to realize a large series of groups of the form PSL(n, q) and PSU(n, q) as Galois groups over Q. We achieve for the first time a fully explicit infinite series of such groups where simultaneously the field can have arbitrarily large degree over the prime field and the group does not coincide with PGL(n, q) or PGU(n, q), respectively.

Mod p Monodromy of Cyclic Covers of the Projective Line

Stepan Nesterov

How does the topology of a family of algebraic curves constrain arithmetic? This paper proves that cyclic covers of the projective line—curves built from nth-root constructions—have 'big monodromy' mod p, meaning their symmetry groups are as large as possible. This generalizes prior results from degrees 2 and 3 to all cyclic covers, with planned applications to constructing explicit Galois extensions of the rationals with prescribed properties.

math.NT math.AG
Original abstract

In this paper, we prove a big monodromy theorem for the monodromy of cyclic coverings of projective line for cohomology with Fp-coefficients. This is a direct generalization of the results of Achter and Pries, where such a theorem is proved for cyclic coverings of degree 2 and 3. Instead of generalizing their methods, we adapt the proof of the analogous theorem for integral cohomology. In our subsequent work, we will apply this theorem to construct in infinitely many cases Galois extensions of Q with Galois group PSL(n, q) and PSU(n, q), where q can be an arbitrarilty large prime power.

Improved bounds for the Fourier uniformity conjecture

Cédric Pilatte

The Liouville function λ(n) encodes the parity of prime factors of n and behaves pseudorandomly. This paper proves that λ has negligible correlation with any linear exponential phase e(nα) when averaged over short intervals of length H≥exp((log X)^(2/5+ε)), improving the previously known threshold and making progress toward the Fourier uniformity conjecture, a key open problem in analytic number theory.

math.NT math.CO
Original abstract

Let $λ$ denote the Liouville function. We prove that $$\sum_{X \leq x < 2X} \sup_{α\in \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}} \bigg\lvert\!\sum_{x \leq n < x+H} λ(n) e(nα)\bigg\rvert = o(HX)$$ as $X\to \infty$, in the regime $H = H(X) \geq \exp((\log X)^{2/5+\varepsilon})$. This improves upon a result of Walsh towards the Fourier uniformity conjecture.

Solution to the Erdos problem on distinct residues of factorials

Vyacheslav M. Abramov

Erdős asked whether any prime p > 5 has all factorials 2!, 3!, …, (p−1)! leaving distinct remainders when divided by p. This paper answers no: no such prime exists. The proof is elementary, meaning it uses only basic modular arithmetic, resolving a clean number-theory question that had remained open despite its simple statement.

math.NT math.CO
Original abstract

Paul Erdos posed the following question: Is there a prime number $p>5$ such that the residues of $2!$, $3!$,\ldots, $(p-1)!$ modulo $p$ all are distinct. In this short note, we give the negative answer on this question in an elementary way.

A structure theorem for sets with doubling $4+δ$

Yifan Jing, Akshat Mudgal

How structured must a set of integers be if it's barely larger than twice its size? This paper extends a classification theorem for sets whose 'doubling constant' (ratio of the sumset size to the original set) is at most 4+δ, pushing past the previously understood threshold of 4. The result inches toward resolving a question by Green about whether highly arithmetic structure—like arithmetic progressions—must emerge near these doubling boundaries.

math.NT math.CO
Original abstract

We prove a structural result for sets of integers with doubling at most $4 + δ$, with $δ>0$ sufficiently small. This generalises earlier work of Eberhard--Green--Manners which dealt with sets of integers with doubling strictly less than $4$, and makes progress towards a question of Green.

Conditional Upper Bounds for Large Deviations and Moments of the Riemann Zeta Function

Louis-Pierre Arguin, Emma Bailey, Asher Roberts

How large can the Riemann zeta function get near the critical line, and how often? Assuming the Riemann Hypothesis, this paper proves tight upper bounds on the probability that |ζ(1/2+it)| exceeds (log T)^k, with a near-optimal logarithmic correction, and recovers Harper's moment bounds as a corollary. The work refines a recursive technique connecting zeta function statistics to branching random walks.

math.NT math.PR
Original abstract

Assuming the Riemann Hypothesis, we show that for $k>0$ $$ \frac{1}{T}\text{meas}\Big\{t\in [T,2T]:|ζ(1/2+{\rm i} t)|>(\log T)^k\Big\}\leq C_k \frac{(\log T)^{-k^2}}{\sqrt{\log\log T}}, $$ where $C_k=\exp(e^{ck})$ for some absolute constant $c>0$. This implies that the $2k$-moments of $|ζ|$ are bounded above by $C_k(\log T)^{k^2}$, recovering the bound of Harper. The proof relies on the recursive scheme of one of the authors with Bourgade and Radziwill (2020), and combines ideas of Soundararajan (2009) and Harper (2013).

Some results on naive transcendence in the ring of integers modulo infinitely large primes

Toshiki Matsusaka, Shin-ichiro Seki

In a nonstandard arithmetic setting called the ring of integers modulo infinitely large primes, there are two competing notions of what it means for an element to be 'transcendental' (not a root of any polynomial with integer coefficients). This paper strengthens earlier results by showing more elements are transcendental in the stricter 'naive' sense, which is harder to establish. The work clarifies the gap between these two transcendence hierarchies in this exotic algebraic structure.

math.NT
Original abstract

This paper presents various transcendence results in the ring of integers modulo infinitely large primes $\mathcal{A}$. In the ring $\mathcal{A}$, one can consider two notions of transcendence. One is based on the notion of finite algebraic numbers introduced by Rosen, while the other is transcendence in the naive sense. It is known that transcendence in the latter sense automatically implies transcendence in the former sense. In this paper, we strengthen results of Anzawa-Funakura and Luca-Zudilin by removing some of their assumptions and, in some cases, upgrading them to statements of naive transcendence. We also present several examples of naive transcendental numbers that do not seem to have appeared previously in the literature. Although we are not able to establish naive transcendence for certain numbers, we prove the irrationality of numbers such as $\log_{\mathcal{A}}(2)$ under the ABC conjecture.

The threshold for linear independence of multiple zeta values in positive characteristic

Bo-Hae Im, Hojin Kim, Tuan Ngo Dac

A 2009 conjecture by Thakur predicted that multiple zeta values (MZVs)—generalizations of Euler's zeta function to function fields over finite fields—of fixed weight are always linearly independent. This paper resolves the conjecture by finding the exact threshold: independence holds through weight 2q, but fails at weight 2q+1 with a unique, explicit relation. This settles a guiding open problem in function field arithmetic.

math.NT
Original abstract

A fundamental conjecture formulated by Thakur in 2009, which has guided significant developments in function field arithmetic, asserts that multiple zeta values (MZV's) in positive characteristic of fixed weight are linearly independent over $\mathbb{F}_q$. In this paper we settle this conjecture by determining the precise threshold for this independence. We prove that linear independence holds for all weights up to 2q, while for weight 2q+1 we establish the existence of a unique and explicit $\mathbb{F}_q$-linear relation. This result provides the first counterexample to Thakur's conjecture. Our proof relies on a new connection between MZVs and Carlitz multiple polylogarithms over $\mathbb{F}_q$, generalizing a central result of [IKLNDP24]. We also introduce a modification of the algorithm from [ND21] that yields a weight-preserving operator acting on $\mathbb{F}_q$-linear relations, providing the algebraic framework for these results.

Reciprocity and the Maslov Phase

Jonathan Holland

Quadratic reciprocity—a classical theorem describing when two primes are 'square roots of each other' modulo one another—gets a new proof here using the Kashiwara–Maslov phase, a quantity from symplectic geometry measuring the geometry of triples of Lagrangian subspaces. The local Hilbert symbol, which encodes quadratic residue information at each prime, emerges naturally as a multiplicativity defect of these phases, giving reciprocity a clean geometric origin.

math.NT math.RT
Original abstract

We give a metaplectic proof of Hilbert reciprocity, and hence of quadratic reciprocity, in which the local phase is the Kashiwara--Maslov phase of a triple of Lagrangians. In rank two the phase of the ordered triple $(L_\infty,L_a,L_0)$ is the one-dimensional Weil index $γ_v(a)$. The local Hilbert symbol appears as the defect of strict multiplicativity of these phases: \[ (a,b)_v = \frac{γ_v(a)γ_v(b)}{γ_v(1)γ_v(ab)}. \] The global step compares the local and adelic realizations of a single Bruhat word for the diagonal torus elements $m(a)\in \operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Q)$. Locally the raw Bruhat-word lift carries the normalization factor determined by the chosen quadratic convention. These operators form a projective representation of the diagonal torus with defect \[ μ_v(a,b) = \frac{γ_v(a)γ_v(b)}{γ_v(1)γ_v(ab)}. \] For rational adelic data, the normalized Bruhat word is multiplicative. The reciprocity law states that the total defect $\prod_vμ_v(a,b)$ is $1$. Combined with the local bridge above, this yields Hilbert reciprocity, while quadratic reciprocity is then the specialization to the pair of odd primes $(p,q)$.

Trilinear Kloosterman fractions I: partially fixed moduli and unbalanced convolutions

Thomas Wright

A central problem in analytic number theory is understanding how arithmetic sequences (like primes) are distributed in residue classes modulo various numbers. This paper sharpens bounds on 'unbalanced convolutions' — sums involving two sequences multiplied together — showing their distribution modulo $q$ is nearly uniform across a wider range than previously known. The improvement uses novel estimates for trilinear exponential sums called Kloosterman fractions and has applications to prime distribution problems.

math.NT
Original abstract

In this paper, we improve on Fouvry and Radziwiłł's results on unbalanced convolutions. In particular, we find that if $(α_m)$ and $(β_n)$ are sequences supported on $m\sim M$ and $n\sim M$ where $β$ is equidistributed for small moduli, then \begin{gather*}\sum_{q\sim Q}\left|\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\substack{n\sim N,m\sim M \\ mn\equiv a\pmod q}}α_mβ_n-\frac{1}{φ(q)}\mathop{\sum\sum}_{\substack{n\sim N,m\sim M \\ (mn,q)=1}}α_mβ_n\right|\ll \frac{X}{\log^A X}, \end{gather*} as long as $\exp((\log x)^{\varepsilon}) \leq N \leq Q^{-11/12} X^{17/36-\varepsilon}$ with $Q\leq X^{1/2+1/66-δ}$, along with wider bounds for $N$ if $Q\leq X^{\frac{45}{89}-ε}$. The former improves the allowable range of $N$, while the latter improves the range of $Q$. To prove these new bounds, we improve Bettin and Chandee's famous result on trilinear forms with Kloosterman fractions in the case where the denominator has a fixed factor.

Elementary anabelian varieties are anabelian

Magnus Carlson

Anabelian geometry asks whether a variety's topology—specifically its fundamental group—completely encodes its geometry. This paper proves that for 'elementary anabelian varieties' (built by iteratively fibering hyperbolic curves) over sub-p-adic fields, isomorphisms and dominant maps between varieties are perfectly captured by corresponding maps of fundamental groups, resolving a significant case of Grothendieck's anabelian conjectures.

math.NT math.AG
Original abstract

We show that isomorphisms of fundamental groups of elementary anabelian varieties -- varieties obtained as iterated fibrations of hyperbolic curves -- over sub-$p$-adic fields correspond bijectively to isomorphisms of varieties. Moreover, dominant maps between proper elementary anabelian varieties are in bijection with ``stably cohomologically injective'' maps of fundamental groups: open maps whose pullbacks to all open subgroups induce injections on cohomology rings with $\ell$-adic coefficients, for any prime $\ell$. This verifies conjectures of Grothendieck from his letter to Faltings. Finally, we establish étale homotopical generalizations of these results.

Joint Sato-Tate Laws for Transformations of Hecke Eigenvalues: The Vertical Case

Mohammad H. Hamdar, Tian Wang

The Sato-Tate conjecture describes how eigenvalues of Hecke operators (arithmetic data attached to modular forms) are distributed statistically. This paper develops a framework with effective error terms for joint equidistribution of transformations of these eigenvalues, introducing a higher-dimensional Erdős-Turán inequality and new techniques involving Hardy-Krause variation to control approximation errors across a broad class of functions.

math.NT
Original abstract

We introduce a framework within which a large class of joint equidistribution problems can be studied and resolved with effective error terms. This involves proving a higher dimensional and $μ$-analogue of the Erdös-Turán inequality, and utilizing the theory of the Hardy-Krause (H-K) variation from analysis, where, in particular, we formulate a technique to approximate a broad class of relevant functions by functions of bounded H-K variation. Our main focus will be on the vertical Sato-Tate problem for spaces of cusp forms and for families of elliptic curves over finite fields. In particular, we obtain novel results concerning the distribution of arithmetic relations, and, more generally, multi-dimensional functions of Fourier coefficients and Frobenius traces.

Non-Vanishing of Cubic Twists of $GL_n(\mathbb{Q})$ $L$-functions

Sayan Ghosh, Pratim Mitra

L-functions are complex-analytic objects encoding deep arithmetic information; twisting them by characters (multiplicative functions) and asking when the result is nonzero is a central problem in number theory. This paper proves that for a broad class of automorphic representations on GL_n, infinitely many cubic twists of the associated L-function are nonzero outside a specific strip in the complex plane. The key advance is removing a restriction to characters of a fixed order that limited all previous results.

math.NT
Original abstract

Let $π$ be an irreducible, cuspidal automorphic representation of $GL_n(\mathbb{A}_\mathbb{Q})$ ($n\geq 3$), which is tempered only for $n=3$. Let $s$ be a complex number such that $\Re(s)\notin \left[1/n, 1-1/n\right]$ if $n\neq 4$; $\Re(s)\notin\left[1/5, 4/5\right]$ if $n=4$, then we show that there are infinitely many primitive cubic Dirichlet characters $χ$ such that $L(s,π\times χ)\neq 0$. Similar results were previously known only for primitive Dirichlet characters without any restriction on the order and quadratic Dirichlet characters.

Valuative independence for Calabi--Yau varieties

Harold Blum, Yuchen Liu

For Calabi–Yau varieties (geometric spaces with trivial canonical bundle, central in mirror symmetry), the paper constructs 'valuatively independent' bases for spaces of functions—bases whose tropicalizations, which track how functions degenerate over a valued field, are as spread out as possible. These bases are expected to match theta functions from mirror symmetry when those exist. The result provides a canonical combinatorial skeleton for CY geometry, bridging algebraic geometry and tropical methods.

math.AG math.DG
Original abstract

We construct valuatively independent bases for the space of sections of an ample line bundle on a log Calabi--Yau pair over a discretely valued field and the space of regular functions on an affine CY pair with maximal boundary. While the bases are not in general unique, they induce canonical functions on the respective skeletons and are expected to agree with tropicalizations of theta functions when they exist. The proof uses techniques from the study of higher rank degenerations in K-stability.

Galois lines for a canonical curve of genus 4, III: non-cyclic Galois lines

Shotaro Kato, Jiryo Komeda, Takeshi Takahashi

Given a genus-4 curve in projective 3-space, a 'Galois line' is a line from which projection onto the projective line yields a Galois field extension. This paper classifies lines whose Galois group is non-cyclic — specifically the symmetric group S₃ or Klein four-group K₄ — completing a trilogy characterizing all Galois lines for such curves. The result gives a full picture of the special geometric structure these curves can possess.

math.AG
Original abstract

Let $C \subset \mathbb{P}^3$ be a canonical curve of genus $4$ over an algebraically closed field $k$ of characteristic zero. For a line $l \subset \mathbb{P}^3$, we consider the projection $π_l: C \to \mathbb{P}^1$ from $l$ and the induced extension of function fields $π_l^*: k(\mathbb{P}^1)\hookrightarrow k(C)$. A line $l$ is called an \emph{$S_3$-line} (resp. a \emph{$K_4$-line}) if the extension $k(C)/π_l^*(k(\mathbb{P}^1))$ is Galois and its Galois group is isomorphic to the symmetric group $S_3$ on three letters (resp. the Klein four-group $K_4$). We prove that the number of $S_3$-lines (resp.\ $K_4$-lines) is at most $10$ (resp.\ $15$).

Compact Kähler contact manifolds

Jie Liu

A Kähler contact manifold is a complex manifold carrying both a Kähler metric (a rich geometric structure) and a contact structure (a type of maximally non-integrable hyperplane field). This paper proves that any *non-projective* compact example must be the projectivized tangent bundle ℙT_Y of some compact Kähler manifold Y, giving a complete structural classification and showing such manifolds have essentially one geometric source.

math.AG math.CV
Original abstract

We prove that a non-projective compact Kähler contact manifold is of the form $\mathbb{P} T_Y$, where $Y$ is a compact Kähler manifold.

Brill-Noether theory for totally ramified covers of the projective line

Daksh Aggarwal

When a curve sits over the projective line with total ramification at two points, what line bundles can live on it? This paper studies 'transmission loci'—subsets of the space of line bundles with prescribed vanishing behavior at those points—and proves dimension formulas and irreducibility results for them, indexed by elements of an affine symmetric group. This refines classical Brill-Noether theory, which counts how many line bundles of given degree and rank exist on a curve.

math.AG
Original abstract

Given a curve $C$ that is a degree $k$ cover $C \to \mathbb{P}^1$ totally ramified at two points $p$ and $q$, we can seek to understand the space of degree $d$ line bundles on $C$ with prescribed ramification at $p$ and $q$. The corresponding subschemes of $\text{Pic}^d(C)$ are called transmission loci and are parameterized via elements of the (extended) $k$-affine symmetric group $\widetildeΣ_k$. Transmission loci provide a refinement of the splitting loci that have recently been extensively studied for $k$-gonal curves. Pflueger has conjectured analogues of the classic Brill-Noether theorem should hold for transmission loci. In this paper we prove Pflueger's conjectures.

Components of strata of k-differentials and their orbit closures

Paul Apisa, Juliet Aygun

Strata of k-differentials are moduli spaces of Riemann surfaces equipped with a k-th root of the canonical bundle, stratified by prescribed zero and pole orders. This paper completely classifies their connected components, finding essentially one 'primitive' nonhyperelliptic component except when k is odd with all even-order singularities—where an Arf invariant distinguishes two—plus new sporadic components for cubic differentials. This closes a long-standing classification problem generalizing earlier results for k=1,2.

math.AG math.DS math.GT
Original abstract

We obtain a complete classification of components of strata of holomorphic and meromorphic k-differentials. We show that, when genus is at least two and outside of explicit exceptions when k < 4, there is one primitive nonhyperelliptic component unless k is odd and all singularities have even order, in which case there are two distinguished by their Arf invariant. The exceptions include new sporadic components of strata of cubic differentials. Our work provides a new proof of earlier results of Kontsevich-Zorich, Boissy, Lanneau, and Chen-Gendron when k = 1, 2. The proofs are almost purely algebraic, relying on the multiscale compactification of Bainbridge-Chen-Gendron-Grushevsky-Moller. This answers a question of Chen-Yu. We also show that for any component of a stratum of finite area $k$-differentials of positive genus, the smallest GL(2,R)-orbit closure containing all of its holonomy covers is as big as possible. This answers the positive genus analogue of a "long term goal" of Mirzakhani-Wright. The result also holds in genus zero strata that contain surfaces with Euclidean cylinders, thus addressing infinitely many cases of the original question of Mirzakhani-Wright.

Florian Pop

The Section Conjecture in anabelian geometry asks whether rational points on a curve correspond exactly to splittings of its fundamental group extension. This paper extends the 't-birational' variant of this conjecture to broader base fields and proves it using surprisingly minimal Galois-theoretic data. The result suggests the conjecture is more robust than previously thought, requiring less arithmetic input to force geometric conclusions.

math.AG

In this note we give generalizations and prove 'minimalistic' refinements of the t-birational Section Conjecture (t-BSC), cf. [Be], by doing both: First, by extending the class of base fields over which the t-BSC holds, and second, by proving refinements of the t-BSC which involve much less, that is minimalistic, Galois theoretical information.

Excess logarithmic residues for foliations by curves and applications

Alana Cavalcante, Maurício Corrêa, Fernando Lourenço et al.

This paper introduces 'excess logarithmic residues' to study holomorphic foliations—geometric structures encoding families of curves on complex manifolds—when those foliations are tangent to a divisor (a hypersurface). These residues measure the discrepancy between two natural ways of computing topological invariants, and yield a new Poincaré-type bound controlling which hypersurfaces can be preserved by the foliation.

math.AG math.CV math.DG
Original abstract

We introduce excess logarithmic residues for one-dimensional holomorphic foliations tangent to a divisor. They arise from the comparison between the logarithmic normal sheaf and the ordinary normal sheaf of the foliation, and measure the local variation between the logarithmic and classical Baum--Bott contributions. We prove a global residue formula expressing the corresponding Chern numbers as sums of local residues. We then derive a Poincaré-type bound for invariant hypersurfaces from the non-negativity of the relevant logarithmic residues. Finally, for a normal \(\mathbb Q\)-Gorenstein surface $Y$, we show that the componentwise logarithmic residues of a lifted foliation along the exceptional divisor of a functorial resolution recover the log discrepancies of the singularities of $Y$, giving a dynamical and foliated test for log canonicity of these singularities.

The $\mathbf{P}^1$-motivic Gysin map

Longke Tang

The Gysin map is a tool in cohomology that pushes classes forward along a closed embedding, crucial for intersection theory. This paper builds a new framework—P¹-motivic spectra without requiring A¹-invariance (so homotopies along the affine line aren't forced to be trivial)—and constructs Gysin maps within it, simultaneously generalizing constructions across many cohomology theories including those not previously covered by standard motivic machinery.

math.AG math.AT math.KT
Original abstract

We develop a $\mathbf{P}^1$-unstable non-$\mathbf{A}^1$-invariant theory of motivic spaces and spectra, and construct the Gysin map therein for regular immersions. This in particular gives the Gysin map in the Annala--Hoyois--Iwasa $\mathbf{P}^1$-motivic spectra, and thus gives a uniform construction for the Gysin maps of various cohomology theories.

Dimension of the motivic Galois group of a 1-motive

Cristiana Bertolin

A 1-motive is an algebraic object encoding relationships between abelian varieties and lattices, and its motivic Galois group measures the hidden symmetries of its periods (special complex numbers). This paper gives an explicit formula for that group's dimension in terms of rank conditions on defining points, and reframes the famous Grothendieck–André Periods Conjecture—which predicts when periods are algebraically independent—in this concrete setting.

Original abstract

We compute the dimension of the motivic Galois group of a 1-motive M defined over the field of complex numbers, expressing it explicitly in terms of the rank of the multiplicative group generated by the points defining M. As an application, we obtain a new formulation of the Grothendieck--André periods Conjecture in the setting of 1-motives.

Birational boundedness of stable families

Paolo Cascini, Jihao Liu, Calum Spicer et al.

In algebraic geometry, a family of varieties is 'birationally bounded' if all members come from finitely many templates up to small modifications. This paper proves that stable families with bounded adjoint volume satisfy this property, using the surprising tool of foliations (geometric structures encoding directions on a variety), establishing a unified framework for classical boundedness problems in higher-dimensional geometry.

math.AG math.DS
Original abstract

We prove that normal projective stable families of maximal variation, of fixed dimension, and with bounded adjoint volume are birationally bounded. This is a consequence of a substantially stronger statement, formulated a priori independently of stable families: algebraically integrable foliations of fixed dimension and bounded adjoint volume are log birationally bounded. In this way, the birational geometry of foliations provides a systematic framework for approaching classical boundedness problems for fibrations. A key input is our proof of M\textsuperscript{c}Kernan's ACC conjecture for interpolated log canonical thresholds of algebraically integrable foliations. This may be viewed as the foliated analogue of Shokurov's ACC conjecture for log canonical thresholds, proved in the classical setting by Hacon--M\textsuperscript{c}Kernan--Xu. As applications, we establish two boundedness criteria for Fano algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structures: Birkar's criterion for exceptional Fanos, and Jiang's criterion for Fanos for which both Tian's $α$-invariant and the anti-canonical volume are bounded away from zero. We also obtain several results on the birational geometry of algebraically integrable adjoint foliated structures, including lower bounds for adjoint volumes, boundedness of automorphism groups, and ACC theorems for pseudo-effective thresholds, $\mathbb{R}$-complementary thresholds, and the Fano spectrum.

A half-space theorem for nonlocal minimal surfaces

Matteo Cozzi, Jack Thompson

A classical theorem by Hoffman and Meeks says two minimal surfaces (area-minimizing surfaces) can't both fit in a half-space without coinciding. This paper proves an analogous result for nonlocal minimal surfaces, where 'area' is measured via a fractional integral capturing long-range interactions. Notably, unlike the classical theorem, this nonlocal version holds in all dimensions, not just low ones.
