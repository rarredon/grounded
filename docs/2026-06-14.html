Welcome to this week's edition of Grounded. It has been a remarkable fortnight for resolution of long-standing open problems: a 55-year-old conjecture of Nash-Williams on triangle decompositions of dense graphs has been settled in full, a decades-old Erdős problem on Ramsey numbers for cycles yields to a clean and general bound, and combinatorialists will want to examine the explicit local bijection finally constructed between alternating sign matrices and descending plane partitions — two families whose equinumerosity has tantalized the field since the 1980s without a satisfying explanation. The second theme is the unexpected appearance of representation-theoretic machinery in probabilistic and combinatorial contexts: Kazhdan–Lusztig *R*-polynomials

Split primes and the Elekes-Rónyai problem

Cosmin Pohoata

The Elekes-Rónyai conjecture predicts that any polynomial in two variables that isn't essentially additive or multiplicative must expand a finite set A to nearly |A|² distinct values. This paper disproves the conjecture by constructing large finite sets A where the polynomial x+y+(x−y)² produces only |A|^(2−c) values for some absolute constant c>0. The counterexample is built using arithmetic properties of split primes.

math.NT math.CO
Original abstract

There exist an absolute constant $c>0$ and arbitrarily large finite sets $A\subset \mathbb{R}$ with $$\left| \left\{x+y+(x-y)^2:\ x, y \in A\right\}\right| \le|A|^{2-c}.$$ Since $x+y+(x-y)^2 \in \mathbb{R}[x,y]$ is a polynomial which is neither additive nor multiplicative, this provides a counterexample for the Elekes-Rónyai problem.

Lower bounds on expressions depending on the functions {\boldmath$\varphi(n)$}, {\boldmath$ψ(n)$} and {\boldmath$σ(n)$}, III

S. I. Dimitrov

How small can combinations of Euler's totient φ(n), the Dedekind psi function ψ(n), and the sum-of-divisors σ(n) get? These three classical arithmetic functions—counting integers coprime to n, summing (1+1/p) over prime divisors, and summing all divisors—satisfy known inequalities, and this paper sharpens those bounds with explicit numerical estimates. Such results matter for analytic number theory, where tight inequalities underpin proofs about multiplicative structure.

math.NT
Original abstract

This work is concerned with the study of lower bounds for various expressions related to the arithmetic functions $\varphi(n)$, $ψ(n)$ and $σ(n)$. Several explicit estimates are established.

The p-adic Cauchy Theorem and Overconvergent Period Sheaves

Finn Wiersig

The classical p-adic Cauchy theorem says that formal power series solutions to p-adic differential equations actually converge—a non-obvious rigidity result in p-adic analysis. This paper proves a geometric generalization: on arbitrary smooth rigid-analytic varieties (p-adic geometric spaces), two seemingly different ways of defining 'horizontal sections' of differential equations—one using overconvergent period sheaves, one using Scholze's OBdR—actually agree. This bridges two major frameworks in p-adic Hodge theory and p-adic differential equations.

math.NT math.AG
Original abstract

The classical p-adic Cauchy theorem asserts that formal solutions of ordinary p-adic differential equations are convergent. In this article we establish a geometric analogue of this result for arbitrary smooth rigid-analytic varieties. More precisely, we show that the horizontal sections functor defined using the overconvergent de Rham period structure sheaf agrees with Scholze's horizontal sections functor defined using OBdR. Equivalently, every formal solution arising from Scholze's construction is already overconvergent. As an application, we identify Scholze's horizontal sections functor with the de Rham functor for D-cap-modules on vector bundles with flat connection.

Rankin--Selberg Subconvexity via Spectral Reciprocity

Peter Humphries, Liyang Yang

L-functions are complex-analytic objects encoding deep arithmetic information, and 'subconvex bounds' give better-than-trivial estimates for their size at the central point. This paper establishes explicit subconvex bounds for Rankin-Selberg L-functions—built from pairs of automorphic forms—over arbitrary number fields, using a sharpened version of spectral reciprocity. The result matters because explicit bounds have direct applications to equidistribution problems in analytic number theory.

math.NT
Original abstract

We establish explicit subconvex bounds for central values of Rankin--Selberg $L$-functions $L(1/2,π\timesπ')$ associated with pairs of unitary cuspidal automorphic representations of $\mathrm{GL}_2$ over a number field. Building on the spectral reciprocity framework of Michel and Venkatesh, we develop a refined, fully explicit form of spectral reciprocity that allows for precise control of conductors and local test vectors. As a consequence, we obtain an explicit subconvex bound, which, even over $F=\mathbb{Q}$, improves all previously known results. We further apply these bounds to several arithmetic problems. These include effective equidistribution of CM suborbits on quaternionic Shimura varieties, quantitative equidistribution of totally geodesic submanifolds, a uniform quantitative form of dihedral quantum unique ergodicity over number fields, and an application to distinguishing cuspidal automorphic representations.

Hilbert irreducibility for algebraic points

Borys Kadets, Danny Neftin

The classical Hilbert irreducibility theorem says that for most rational points, a polynomial stays irreducible over ℚ. This paper asks an algebraic-geometry version: for a map between curves, when do only finitely many algebraic points of a fixed degree have reducible preimages? The authors prove broad conditions guaranteeing this finiteness, extending Hilbert irreducibility to higher-degree algebraic points.

math.NT math.AG
Original abstract

We study the following problem: given a covering of curves $φ\colon X \to X_0$ over a number field $k$, and an integer $d$, when is the set \[\{p \in X_0(\overline{k})|\ \mathrm{deg}\ p = d, \text{ and the fiber } φ^{-1}(p) \text{ is reducible over } k(p)\}\] finite? In case $X$ itself admits infinitely many degree $d$ points, we consider the modified problem where the images of degree $d$ points on $X$ are removed from the set. We prove a number of theorems ensuring a positive answer. As a consequence we show that for a fixed curve $X$ and all sufficiently high-degree indecomposable rational functions $φ:X \to \mathbb{P}^1$ with $b$ branch points, the set of reducible fibers above degree $d<b/7-2$ points, not containing a degree $d$ point from $X$, is finite.

A generic categorical local Langlands correspondence for quasi-split reductive groups

David Helm, Maarten Solleveld, Yujie Xu

The Local Langlands correspondence is a deep bridge between representations of p-adic groups and arithmetic objects called L-parameters. This paper proves a 'categorical' version — upgrading the correspondence from individual objects to entire categories — for quasi-split reductive groups including all classical groups. The fully faithful functor constructed here is a major step toward the full geometric Local Langlands program.

math.NT math.AG math.RT
Original abstract

We prove a generic categorical (arithmetic) local Langlands conjecture for a large class of quasi-split reductive $p$-adic groups $G$, including all quasi-split classical groups and some non-classical groups. More precisely, we construct a natural fully faithful functor from the stable $\infty$-category of generic Bernstein blocks on the automorphic side to the stable $\infty$-category of ind-coherent sheaves on the moduli stack of (arithmetic) $L$-parameters, generalizing earlier work of [BZCHN24] for $\mathrm{GL}_n$. Moreover, for an arbitrary quasi-split reductive $p$-adic group $G$, we formulate a classical local Langlands framework under which a classical correspondence can be lifted to an $\infty$-categorical correspondence. Furthermore, combined with the recent work of Hansen-Mann [HM26] and assuming the expected compatibility of Fargues-Scholze construction with spectral Eisenstein series, our results give the full Fargues--Scholze categorical local Langlands equivalence [FS24], without the genericity condition, for a large class of quasi-split reductive $p$-adic groups $G$.

Maximal Transcendentality of the Double-Scaled PCM

Evgeny Sobko

The Principal Chiral Model is a quantum field theory whose perturbative expansion produces real numbers called 'transcendental' (involving π, zeta values, etc.), and 'maximal transcendentality' means these numbers are as complex as possible at each order. This paper proves, to all orders, that the vacuum energy in a specific large-N double-scaling limit satisfies this principle, with coefficients being rational polynomials in odd zeta values. Hidden numerical patterns in the first 35 computed orders hint at deeper structure still to be explained.

hep-th math-ph math.NT
Original abstract

We prove, to all orders, maximal transcendentality of the strongly coupled large-N Principal Chiral Model in the double-scaling regime introduced in our earlier work. We also prove that, after a natural shift of the coupling constant, the coefficients of the vacuum-energy expansion are expressed purely as polynomials in odd zeta values with rational coefficients. The first 35 explicitly computed orders reveal further number-theoretic regularities, pointing to hidden structure beyond maximal transcendentality.

Fine-scale statistics for $\mathbb{Q}^n$

Gaurav Aggarwal, Anish Ghosh, Jens Marklof

How are rational numbers with nearly equal denominators distributed in space? This paper analyzes 'fine-scale statistics' — subtle spacing patterns among rational points in ℝⁿ whose denominators fall in a narrow window — going far beyond classical Farey sequences where the window is as wide as the denominator itself. The limiting distributions are expressed using affine lattices (integer lattice translates), extending our understanding of how arithmetic structure shapes geometric distribution.

math.NT math.DS math.PR
Original abstract

We study the distribution of rational points in $\mathbb{R}^n$, with denominators restricted to the interval $[Q-Δ, Q]$, and $Q,Δ\to\infty$ such that $Δ/Q\to 0$. Previous results in the literature, due to Hall and others, were limited to Farey sequences, where the window size $Δ$ is of the same order as $Q$. We prove the convergence of fine-scale statistics in a range of scaling limits and express the limit laws in terms of natural probability measures on the space of affine lattices. The key technical ingredient of our approach is an equidistribution theorem for slowly expanding horospheres, with some new exotic limit measures. Our techniques furthermore allow us to answer a recent question by Anderson, Boca, Cobeli and Zaharescu concerning the directional statistics of lattice points.

Multiple zeta values ending with a fixed string

Steven Charlton

Multiple zeta values — generalizations of Riemann's zeta function involving nested sums — satisfy intricate algebraic relations organized by weight, depth, and height. This paper gives a generating series formula for sums of all multiple zeta values sharing fixed weight, depth, and a prescribed ending string, extending the Ohno–Zagier theorem and yielding new evaluations of interpolated multiple zeta values.

math.NT
Original abstract

We give a generating series expression for the sum of all multiple zeta values of a fixed weight, depth, and height, which end with a given string $\vec{\ell} = (\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_r)$; this builds upon the proof of the Ohno-Zagier Theorem. In particular, the sum of all multiple zeta values of fixed weight, depth and ending with $\vec{\ell}$ has bounded depth $\leq \ell_1 + \cdots + \ell_r$. We give some applications to evaluations of interpolated multiple zeta values, and to the generating series of double zeta values.

On the Smallest Counterexample to the Log-Concavity of the D'Arcais Polynomials

Steven Charlton, Bernhard Heim, Johann Stumpenhusen

D'Arcais polynomials arise in partition theory, and a conjecture predicted they are log-concave (meaning their coefficients satisfy aₖ² ≥ aₖ₋₁aₖ₊₁). This paper refines earlier asymptotic arguments disproving that conjecture to pinpoint the first explicit counterexample at λ = 65,214,507,758,400 — a 14-digit number — and analyzes how frequently such failures occur asymptotically.

math.NT
Original abstract

Recently, Starr used asymptotic methods to disprove a conjecture by Heim--Neuhauser and Abdesselam about the log-concavity of the D'Arcais polynomials, without giving an explicit counterexample. We refine the asymptotics, to give the necessary estimates on convolutions of $σ_{-1}$, and identify the first counterexample at $λ= 65\,214\,507\,758\,400$. We also consider the asymptotic density of such counterexamples.

Abelian surfaces of small conductor from genus 3 double covers

Raymond van Bommel, Céline Maistret, Jia Shi et al.

Abelian surfaces are two-dimensional generalizations of elliptic curves, and their 'conductor' measures arithmetic complexity at primes where they behave badly. This paper builds a database of roughly 500,000 abelian surfaces over the rationals with small conductor, constructed via Prym varieties — geometric objects arising from double covers of curves — giving researchers a rich testbed for studying these higher-dimensional arithmetic objects.

math.NT
Original abstract

We describe the construction of a database of roughly half a million abelian surfaces over Q of small conductor arising as Pryms associated to a genus 3 double cover of a genus 1 curve. Our construction uses a method that provides a degree of control over the primes of bad reduction.

Insufficiency of the algebraic Brauer--Manin obstruction for homogeneous spaces

Nguyen Manh Linh

The Brauer–Manin obstruction is a tool for explaining why a variety can have points over every local field (real numbers, p-adic numbers) yet no global rational point. This paper constructs the first example where this obstruction for a homogeneous space comes from a 'transcendental' Brauer class—one invisible to purely algebraic methods—proving that the algebraic Brauer–Manin obstruction alone is insufficient to explain all such failures.

math.NT
Original abstract

Over any number field containing a root of unity of odd prime order, we construct a homogeneous space of $\mathrm{SL}_n$ with finite $2$-nilpotent geometric stabilizers, with a constant unramified algebraic Brauer group, which has no rational point but has local points in every completion of the ground field. This yields the first example of transcendental Brauer--Manin obstruction for homogeneous spaces of connected linear algebraic groups. Our method exploits a previous idea by Borovoi and Kunyavskii.

Fourier Coefficients of Siegel-Eisenstein Series of Degree $2m$ and Weight $m+1$

Nobuki Takeda

Siegel-Eisenstein series are higher-dimensional generalizations of classical modular forms, and their Fourier coefficients encode deep arithmetic data. This paper explicitly computes the Fourier coefficients of a specific Siegel-Eisenstein series of degree 2m and weight m+1, generalizing known degree-two formulas to much higher dimensions. The results extend work of Kohnen and Nagaoka to a regime where such explicit computations were previously unavailable.

math.NT
Original abstract

We study Fourier coefficients of the Siegel-Eisenstein series $E_{m+1}^{(2m)}(Z)$ for $m\equiv1\pmod4$ and $m\geq5$. Using the Fourier expansion formula due to Mizumoto, we determine the constant term and the exceptional non-zero Fourier coefficients. The result gives a higher-degree analogue of the degree-two formulas of Kohnen and Nagaoka, which were later rederived by Haruki.

The Dirichlet spectrum with respect to $L_1$ norm is $\left[\frac12,1\right]$

Nikita Shulga

The Dirichlet spectrum measures how well real numbers can be approximated by rationals under various norms. This paper pins down the full Dirichlet spectrum for L1-norm approximation as exactly the interval [1/2, 1], resolving an open problem, and equivalently characterizes the Minkowski spectrum—arising from a continued-fraction algorithm—as [1/4, 1/2]. The proof also describes the fractal-like structure of level sets where the Minkowski constant takes a fixed value.

math.NT
Original abstract

We prove that the one-dimensional Dirichlet spectrum with respect to approximation in $L_1$ norm $\mathbb{D}^{[1]}$ satisfies $$ \mathbb{D}^{[1]}=\left[\frac12,1\right]. $$ This is equivalent to the fact that the Minkowski spectrum $\mathbb M$, associated with the Minkowski diagonal continued fraction, satisfies $$ \mathbb M=\left[\frac14,\frac12\right]. $$ Further, we show that level sets $$ Θ_m=\{α\in(0,1)\setminus\mathbb Q:\mathfrak m(α)=m\}, $$ where $\mathfrak{m}(α)$ is the Minkowski constant of $α$, have Hausdorff dimension strictly greater than $1/2$ for any $m\in(1/4,1/2]$, while $\dim_H Θ_{1/4}=\frac{1}{2}$.

Extremal Type II lattices of rank $72$ are generated by their second shell

Scott Duke Kominers

An extremal Type II lattice is a maximally sphere-packed even, self-dual lattice—rank 72 is one of three mysterious cases where such objects might exist. This paper proves that any such rank-72 lattice is entirely spanned by its shortest nontrivial vectors (norm 10), using the fact that those vectors form a spherical 11-design. This structural constraint could help efforts to classify or construct these elusive, still-hypothetical lattices.

math.NT math.CO math.MG
Original abstract

We show that if $L$ is an extremal Type II lattice of rank $72$, then $L$ is generated by its vectors of norm $10$. The proof determines the full inner product distribution between the shells of norms $8$ and $10$ using the spherical $11$-design property of the norm-$10$ shell.

On the degree-$2$ Siegel theta series of extremal even unimodular lattices of ranks $48$, $72$, $96$, and $120$

Scott Duke Kominers

Siegel theta series are higher-dimensional generalizations of classical theta functions that encode the geometry of lattices. This paper shows that for extremal even unimodular lattices in ranks 48, 72, and 96, the degree-2 Siegel theta series is uniquely determined by the lattice's rank alone, with rank 120 being the first exception. The result gives clean genus-2 proofs using a new 'depth filtration' on cusp forms tied to how extremality forces Fourier coefficients to vanish.

math.NT math.CO
Original abstract

We study degree-$2$ Siegel theta series of extremal even unimodular lattices from the genus-$2$ viewpoint initiated by Ozeki. Using Igusa's structure theorem, we define a depth filtration on genus-$2$ cusp forms, measured by the total degree in $χ_{10}$ and $χ_{12}$, and relate it to the vanishing of low Fourier--Jacobi coefficients forced by extremality. In ranks $48$, $72$, and $96$, this interaction closes exactly and yields a direct genus-$2$ proof that the degree-$2$ theta series is uniquely determined by extremality (conditional on existence in rank $96$). In rank $120$ (again conditional on existence), the same argument leaves a one-dimensional residual line spanned by $χ_{10}^6$: with $χ_{10}$ in the standard integral normalization, any two such degree-$2$ theta series differ by an integer multiple of $χ_{10}^6$.

Uncertainty Principles for the Number Theoretic Transform

Giulio Malavolta, Alon Rosen

The number-theoretic transform (NTT) is a finite-field analogue of the discrete Fourier transform used in cryptography and polynomial arithmetic. This paper proves an uncertainty principle for the NTT: a nonzero function and its transform cannot both be sparse, with their combined support sizes summing to at least q+1—the maximum possible. This tight bound, analogous to the classical Donoho-Stark uncertainty principle, has direct implications for polynomial identity testing algorithms.

math.NT cs.CR cs.DS
Original abstract

Motivated by polynomial identity testing with exponentials (Li and Wu, ITCS'26), we study uncertainty principles for the number-theoretic transform (NTT). We show that the NTT satisfies strong sparsity tradeoffs: For every fixed prime $q$ and for all but finitely many primes $p \equiv 1 \pmod q$ every nonzero $f\in \mathbb F_p^{\mathbb Z_q}$ and its number-theoretic transform $\hat f$ satisfy \[ |\mathrm{Supp}(f)| + |\mathrm{Supp}(\hat f)| \ge q+1. \] Thus, a $k$-sparse function has transform support at least $q-k+1$. As our main technical contribution, we prove a probabilistic version of the above uncertainty principle, averaged over primes $p$, in the regime $p=q^{O(1)}$. As an application, we obtain a black-box identity test for $k$-sparse exponential polynomials of degree at most $d$ with vanishing soundness error, for $q$ moderately larger than $k$.

Fence Complexes and Toric Degenerations of Positroid Varieties

Cameron Chang, Pranav Enugandla, Josephine Hlavinka

Positroid varieties are algebraic subvarieties of the Grassmannian (the space of k-dimensional subspaces of n-dimensional space) with rich combinatorial structure. This paper constructs 'fence complexes'—polyhedral regions built from faces of a classical polytope—that encode each positroid variety, proves they're topologically balls, and uses them to build toric degenerations. The result gives explicit combinatorial tools for studying these geometrically important varieties.

math.AG math.CO
Original abstract

We associate to each positroid variety in the Grassmannian $\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)$ a polyhedral complex, which we call a fence complex. Fence complexes consist of unions of faces of the Gelfand-Tsetlin polytope $P_{k,n}$ associated to a fundamental weight $ω_k$. We show that these fence complexes are homeomorphic to closed balls. Furthermore, they endow the Gelfand-Tsetlin polytope with the structure of a regular CW complex, giving a polyhedral complex presentation of the regular CW complex structure on $\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)_{\geq 0}$. We also show that the Ehrhart polynomial of a fence complex equals the Hilbert polynomial of the associated positroid variety. We prove that under the Sturmfels-Gonciulea-Lakshmibai degeneration of $\mathrm{Gr}(k,n)$ to the toric variety of the Gelfand-Tsetlin polytope, positroid varieties degenerate to the reduced union of toric varieties corresponding to their fence complexes. As an application, we classify when positroid varieties contained inside hook Schubert varieties are arithmetically Gorenstein. We also derive a recursive character formula for cyclic Demazure modules, which we show is equivalent to a formula of Almousa, Gao and Huang.

Algebraic cobordism rings of wonderful varieties and matroids

Raj Gandhi, Ethan Partida

Algebraic cobordism is a refined cohomology theory for algebraic varieties that captures more arithmetic information than the classical Chow ring. This paper gives combinatorial formulas for the cobordism ring of toric varieties attached to matroids (combinatorial abstractions of linear independence) and proves it splits as a tensor product with the Chow ring, extending a known isomorphism from geometry to the broader matroid setting.

math.AG math.CO
Original abstract

We give two combinatorial presentations for the algebraic cobordism ring $Ω^*(M)$ of the toric variety of the Bergman fan of any loopless matroid $M$. As a consequence of our presentations, we obtain an $Ω^*(\mathrm{pt})$-algebra isomorphism $Ω^*(M) \simeq CH^*(M) \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} Ω^*(\mathrm{pt})$, where $CH^*(M)$ is the Chow ring of $M$ and $Ω^*(\mathrm{pt})$ is the algebraic cobordism ring of the point. This isomorphism generalizes, in part, the exceptional integral isomorphism between the Chow ring and $K$-ring of a matroid, studied in the recent works of Berget--Eur--Spink--Tseng and Larson--Li--Payne--Proudfoot. For a complex hyperplane arrangement $\mathcal{H}$, we prove that the algebraic cobordism ring of the wonderful variety $W_\mathcal{H}$ of $\mathcal{H}$ and the algebraic cobordism ring of the toric variety of the matroid underlying $\mathcal{H}$ are isomorphic, and that both rings coincide with the complex cobordism ring of $W_\mathcal{H}$.

Functorial stratifications of singularities in characteristic 0

Vicente Monreal

Stratifications decompose a singular algebraic variety into smooth pieces, but classical methods can depend on arbitrary choices. This paper shows that a recently introduced 'riso-stratification'—defined using model theory and non-archimedean geometry—is actually canonical and functorial, meaning it respects maps between spaces and works intrinsically. This bridges abstract logical methods with mainstream algebraic geometry over characteristic-zero fields.

math.AG math.LO
Original abstract

We prove that the riso-stratifications of singularities of affine algebraic sets introduced by Bradley-Williams--Halupczok are embedding independent and can be computed étale locally. Building on this, we upgrade their procedure, originally formulated in non-archimedean and model-theoretic language, into a sharp canonical and functorial stratification process for schemes of finite type over fields of characteristic 0. Our work enables comparisons with classical approaches to singularities and makes these stratifications available without requiring familiarity with the original methods.

Moduli of Supersingular Abelian Varieties in Dimensions $g\leq 5$

Michael Burger

Supersingular abelian varieties are special geometric objects that arise in characteristic p arithmetic geometry, and understanding their moduli space (the space parametrizing all such objects) is an open challenge. This paper develops explicit classification tools for dimensions up to 5 by analyzing structured geometric objects called polarized flag type quotients, giving computable descriptions of the fibers of the moduli map.

math.AG math.NT
Original abstract

We establish structure theorems for polarised flag type quotients arising in the study of the moduli space of supersingular abelian varieties. In particular, we prove a refined normal form for the top level of these flags and derive an explicit, computable descent criterion for quasi-polarisations. These results provide a complete classification of the first and last step of these flags. As an application, we compute polarised flag type quotients in dimensions $g\leq 5$, describing the fibers of the truncation morphisms as classification objects of supersingular abelian varieties.

Kuznetsov components and transcendental motives of cubic fourfolds

Claudio Pedrini

Cubic fourfolds (degree-3 hypersurfaces in 5-dimensional projective space) carry two sophisticated invariants: a Kuznetsov component (a triangulated subcategory of coherent sheaves) and a transcendental motive (capturing cohomological complexity). This paper shows that for special families of cubic fourfolds related by Fourier-Mukai equivalence—a derived-category duality—these invariants stay linked, deepening connections between derived categories and motivic geometry.

math.AG
Original abstract

Let $X \subset ¶^5_{\C}$ be a smooth cubic fourfold.The Kuznetsov component $\sA_X$ is contained in the derived category $D^b(X)$ and the transcendental motive $t(X)$ is contained in the category of Chow motives $\sM_{rat}(\C))$. If $X$ and $Y$ are {\it Fourier -Mukai partners} and hence the categories $\sA_X$ and $\sA_Y$ are equivalent, then their transcendental motives $t(X)$ and $t(Y)$ are isomorphic. The aim of this note is to consider families of special cubic fourfolds $X$ with their FM-partners $Y$ and to give an explicit description of the isomorphism between the transcendental motives, in the case $X$ and $Y$ are rational and when they are conjecturally irrational. We also prove that ,for special cubic fourfolds $X $ in countably many Hassett divisors, with a symplectic automorphism of order 3, there exists another special cubic fourfold $Y$, an equivalence of categories $\sA^G_X \simeq \sA_{Y}$, where $\sA^G_X$ is the equivariant Kuznetsov component, and an isomorphism $t(X) \simeq t(Y)$.

Stratification of moduli spaces of instantons on the Segre product of three lines via 't Hooft bundles

Vincenzo Antonelli, Francesco Malaspina

Instanton bundles are algebraic vector bundles on algebraic varieties generalizing gauge-theoretic instantons from physics, and their moduli spaces—parameter spaces collecting all such bundles—can be complex and hard to navigate. Working on the product of three projective lines, this paper introduces special sub-families called 't Hooft bundles to build a natural stratification (a layered decomposition) of these moduli spaces. The authors describe the geometry explicitly using Serre correspondence and Hilbert schemes, giving concrete structure to an otherwise unwieldy moduli problem.

math.AG
Original abstract

Let $X$ be the Segre product of three projective lines. For a fixed effective divisor $D$ on $X$, we introduce the notions of $D$-'t Hooft, $(D_i,D_j)$-special and $D$-sectional special bundle. The varieties parameterizing these bundles yield a natural stratification of the moduli space of stable instanton bundles with fixed Chern classes. After characterizing the curves associated with these bundles via Serre correspondence, we describe the corresponding Hilbert schemes. Using this description, we analyze the moduli spaces of $h_i$-'t Hooft bundles and the smaller strata of $(h_i,h_j)$-special and $(h_i)$-sectional special bundles. Finally, we provide a detailed study of the low-charge cases.

Answer to a decomposition question on tori raised by Colliot-Thélène and Sansuc

Anis Zidani

Algebraic tori are group varieties generalizing the multiplicative group, and Colliot-Thélène and Sansuc asked in 1987 whether they always admit a certain decomposition involving flasque tori. This paper answers that question negatively, constructing an explicit torus T over the rationals and a prime p where the product of T's p-adic integer points and rational points fails to cover all p-adic points. The counterexample has concrete arithmetic consequences for local-global principles.

math.AG math.NT
Original abstract

The aim of this note is to present a simple strategy to answer negatively a decomposition question on tori posed by Colliot-Thélène and Sansuc in the article \textit{Principal Homogeneous Spaces under Flasque Tori: Applications} of 1987. We then deduce a torus $T$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ and a prime number $p$ such that $T(\mathbb{Z}_p)\,T(\mathbb{Q})\not=T(\mathbb{Q}_p)$, where $T(\mathbb{Z}_p)$ denotes the maximal compact subgroup of $T(\mathbb{Q}_p)$.

The convergent stack

Marco D'Addezio

Convergent cohomology is a tool for studying algebraic varieties in positive characteristic (like mod-p geometry) using p-adic analysis. This paper introduces a unified 'convergent stack' associated to any scheme over 𝔽ₚ — a geometric object that packages convergent isocrystals (coefficient systems for this cohomology) as plain sheaves, making them easier to manipulate and connecting naturally to related theories like de Rham and crystalline cohomology.

math.AG math.NT
Original abstract

Inspired by Simpson's de Rham stack and Drinfeld's crystalline stack, we develop a stacky approach to convergent cohomology and convergent isocrystals in positive characteristic. To any scheme $X$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$ we attach a convergent stack $X_{\mathrm{conv}}$. When $X$ is of finite type over a perfect field, its finitely generated quasi-coherent $\mathcal{O}[\tfrac1p]$-modules are equivalent to convergent isocrystals over $X$, compatibly with cohomology.
