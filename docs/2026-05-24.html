Arboreal Galois Groups of a PCF Map with Strictly Pre-periodic Critical Points

Özlem Ejder, Zofia Gołaska, Yasemin Kara et al.

When you repeatedly apply a rational function, the symmetries of its iteration tree form a 'Galois group' capturing deep arithmetic structure. For the specific map f(x)=2/(x-1)², whose critical points eventually settle without cycling, the authors explicitly describe these groups and prove the arithmetic version has Hausdorff dimension zero—meaning it's surprisingly small. This advances understanding of which PCF maps produce 'small' arboreal Galois groups, a central open question in arithmetic dynamics.

math.NT math.GR
Original abstract

We study the arithmetic and geometric iterated monodromy groups associated to the postcritically finite (PCF) quadratic rational function $f(x)=\frac{2}{(x-1)^2}$ defined over a number field $k$, whose critical points are both strictly pre-periodic. We give explicit recursive descriptions of the topological generators of the geometric iterated monodromy group of $f$ and show that the arithmetic iterated monodromy group has Hausdorff dimension zero. We describe an explicit criterion to determine the values $a\in k$ for which the associated arboreal Galois group achieves its maximum possible size. In particular, we show that maximality of the arboreal Galois group can already be verified at level four, which is computationally accessible. Finally, we determine the intersection of the constant field of the arithmetic iterated monodromy group with $k(μ_{2^{\infty}})$, providing the first full study of a PCF quadratic map with non-abelian constant field.

On the structure of fine Mordell-Weil groups over a $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension and its intermediate subextensions

Meng Fai Lim

The fine Mordell-Weil group strips away p-power torsion from an elliptic curve's rational points, giving a cleaner object for studying Iwasawa theory—a framework for understanding arithmetic over infinite towers of number fields. This paper analyzes how these fine groups behave across the intermediate layers of a ℤₚ-extension (an infinite Galois extension with Galois group isomorphic to the p-adic integers), clarifying their algebraic structure as one climbs the tower.

Escaping Chaos in Random Multiplicative Functions

Max Wenqiang Xu

A Steinhaus random multiplicative function assigns independent random complex values to primes and multiplies them together. The paper asks: when does a partial sum over a set A normalize to a standard complex Gaussian? The answer is exactly when A has density zero among integers up to N — and this density threshold is sharp. This pins down precisely how 'spread out' a set must be before random multiplicative functions behave like independent noise.

math.NT math.PR
Original abstract

Let $f(n)$ be a Steinhaus random multiplicative function. Let $A\subset [1, N]$ be a finite set of integers. We show that \[\frac{1}{\sqrt{|A|}} \sum_{n\in A} f(n) \xrightarrow[]{d} \mathcal{CN}(0,1)\] forces that $|A|=o(N)$. We prove that the $o(1)$ density is sharp by showing that for most sets $A$, and thus confirm the existence, with density $ρ$ such that $(1-ρ)^{-1} =o((\log \log N)^{1/2})$, we have \[ \frac{1}{\sqrt{(1-ρ) |A|}} \sum_{n\in A} f(n) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{CN}(0,1). \] The extra factor $\sqrt{1-ρ}$ makes a difference as long as the density $ρ>0$.

Sharp omega results for the divisor and circle problems

Youness Lamzouri

The divisor problem and circle problem ask how large the error term can be when counting lattice points; an omega result gives a provable lower bound on this fluctuation. This paper establishes omega results matching the conjectured truth and, crucially, determines the sign of the extremes — improving Hafner's 1981 records for the first time. The key innovation is a resonance method applied directly to the oscillatory phase of the underlying exponential sums.

math.NT
Original abstract

We establish omega results for the divisor and circle problems that are conjecturally sharp, while also determining the sign of the large values obtained. This improves on the work of Soundararajan and on the subsequent independent refinements of Sourmelidis and Mahatab, and gives the first improvement on Hafner's 1981 $Ω_+$ result for the divisor problem and his $Ω_-$ result for the circle problem. The main new ingredient is a resonance method which works directly with the phase appearing in the Voronoï summation formula. This is achieved by replacing the usual positive kernels by a one-sided sectorial kernel, namely the density of a Gamma distribution, whose Fourier transform lies in a suitable sector of the complex plane.

Generalized Fermat equation over cyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_l$-extensions of totally real fields

Satyabrat Sahoo

Fermat's Last Theorem has been generalized to ask whether equations like Ax^p + By^p + Cz^p = 0 have nontrivial solutions over number fields beyond the rationals. This paper proves the asymptotic version—no solutions for sufficiently large primes p—over the layers of cyclotomic Z_l-extensions of certain totally real fields, under explicit arithmetic conditions including a non-Wieferich prime hypothesis. This pushes Fermat-type results into an infinite tower of number fields.

math.NT
Original abstract

Let $K$ be a totally real number field of odd degree. Let $l \geq 5$ be a prime with $l \nmid [K:\mathbb{Q}]$ and $\gcd(\frac{l-1}{2}, [K:\mathbb{Q}])=1$. We prove that if $2$ is inert in $K$, $l$ is non-Wieferich, i.e., $2^{l-1} \not\equiv 1 \pmod{l^2}$, and $l$ is totally ramified in $K$, then the asymptotic Fermat's Last Theorem holds over each $n$-th layer $K_{n,l}$ of the cyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_l$-extension of $K$. We then prove that the generalized Fermat equation $Ax^p+By^p+Cz^p=0$ has no asymptotic solution over each $n$-th layer $K_{n,l}$ when $A,B,C \in \{u2^r : u\in \mathcal{O}_K^\times,\ r \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}$. For any odd prime $d$, we also prove that if $A,B,C \in \{\pm 2^r d^s : r,s \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\}$ and $h_{\mathbb{Q}_{n,l}}^+$ is odd, then the generalized Fermat equation $Ax^p+By^p+Cz^p=0$ has no effective asymptotic solution $(a,b,c) \in \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}_{n,l}}^3$ with $2 \mid abc$. The effectivity in the case of $\mathbb{Q}_{n,l}$ follows from a result of Throne proving the modularity of elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}_{n,l}$.

The inhomogeneous Khintchine Theorem in dimension two

Demi Allen, Manuel Hauke-Treuer, Felipe A. Ramírez

Khintchine's Theorem classifies which real numbers can be well-approximated by rationals at a given rate, and its inhomogeneous variant asks the same question with a fixed offset added. The monotonicity assumption on the approximation function was known to be dispensable in three or more dimensions but required in one dimension, leaving dimension two open for decades — this paper resolves that final case, proving monotonicity is unnecessary in two dimensions and completing the metric theory of inhomogeneous Diophantine approximation.

math.NT
Original abstract

We prove that the inhomogeneous variant of Khintchine's Theorem holds in dimension $2$ without any monotonicity assumption. This resolves the last remaining case in the metric theory of inhomogeneous Diophantine approximation: while the monotonicity assumption is known to be unnecessary in dimensions $m\geq 3$ and necessary in dimension $m=1$, the two-dimensional case has remained open. It also settles the final outstanding case of a Khintchine--Groshev-type theorem for the approximation of systems of linear forms, confirming a conjecture of the first and third authors. Our results bring the inhomogeneous theory of metric Diophantine approximation into alignment with its homogeneous counterpart.

Two determinant evaluations in Sun's conjectures involving Legendre symbols

Yaoran Yang, Yutong Zhang

Legendre symbols encode quadratic residues modulo a prime p, and Sun conjectured specific formulas for determinants of matrices built from them. This paper proves two such conjectures by connecting the determinants to known number-theoretic quantities involving sums of two squares, resolving the p≡1 (mod 4) case of one conjecture and another related identity.

math.NT
Original abstract

We prove two determinant evaluations attached to Sun's conjectures on matrices of Legendre symbols. The first one resolves the \(p\equiv1\pmod4\) part of Conjecture 4.8(i) by reducing the determinant with four indeterminates to a four-entry inverse package for the adjacent minor \([χ(j-k+1)]_{0\le j,k<(p-1)/2}\). The core evaluation is \[ \det H=\leg{2}{p}(b'_p-a'_p),\qquad U^TH^{-1}U= \begin{pmatrix} \leg{2}{p}\dfrac{pb'_p-a'_p}{b'_p-a'_p}&1\\[2mm] \dfrac{b'_p-a'_p-1}{b'_p-a'_p}&1 \end{pmatrix}, \] where \(U=(\mathbf1,η)\) and \(η_j=χ(j)\). The proof uses Vsemirnov's factorisation of Chapman's matrix and an adjacent cofactor calculation. The second result resolves Conjecture 4.10(i) for \(p\equiv1\pmod4\) in a stronger form: for any ordered half-system modulo sign and all \(u,v\in\mathbb F_p\), the determinant is evaluated exactly modulo \(p\). This second proof is a two-antidiagonal Vandermonde factorisation, and it gives the asserted square class as an immediate corollary.

Halász theorems for Gaussian ideals in sectors and short intervals

Jan Kuś

How do multiplicative functions—number-theoretic functions respecting multiplication—behave when summed over ideals in the Gaussian integers ℤ[i], which encode arithmetic in the complex plane? This paper proves a quantitative version of Halász's theorem in that setting, controlling sums by how 'pretentious' (close) a function is to simple power functions, and extends results to ideals in angular sectors and short intervals. The work generalizes classical analytic number theory to a richer algebraic setting.

math.NT
Original abstract

We prove a quantitative Halász theorem for multiplicative functions on the nonzero ideals of $\mathbb{Z}[i]$, with bounds controlled by pretentious distance to the Archimedean characters $N^{it}$. We also prove a sectorial analogue: under angular non-pretentiousness, the sum of $f$ over ideals lying in a fixed sector is asymptotically given by the expected proportion of the unrestricted sum. Finally, under angular non-pretentiousness and a non-degeneracy condition on conjugate prime pairs, we prove a sectorial short-interval version of the Halász theorem for annular sectors whose radial thickness tends to infinity. The proof of the sectorial short-interval Halász theorem uses angular Fourier expansion, norm-compression to multiplicative functions on $\mathbb{N}$, and a theorem of Mangerel.

Short Salem polynomials

James McKee, Chris Smyth

Salem numbers are algebraic integers greater than 1 whose conjugates all lie on or inside the unit circle—they appear in geometry, topology, and dynamics. This paper classifies all Salem polynomials (whose roots are Salem numbers) of length 5 and nearly classifies length-6 ones, finding 12 infinite families plus finitely many exceptions. The work connects to Lehmer's Conjecture, a 90-year-old open question about the smallest possible growth rate of such numbers.

math.NT
Original abstract

We give a complete classification of all Salem polynomials of length 5. For length 6 we show that all but finitely many Salem polynomials lie in one of 12 infinite families, and subject to Lehmer's Conjecture we give a complete list of the 126 exceptions. We provide a table of short polynomials for all known Salem numbers below the smallest Pisot number.

Linear equations in Piatetski-Shapiro primes

Xuancheng Shao, Yu-Chen Sun

Piatetski-Shapiro primes are primes of the form ⌊n^(1/γ)⌋ — a sparse, structured subset of the primes. This paper proves these primes are 'uncorrelated' with nilsequences (oscillating functions built from polynomial sequences on Lie groups), then uses this to count solutions to systems of linear equations within this prime set. It extends additive combinatorics results like the Green-Tao theorem to this more exotic class of primes.

math.NT math.CO
Original abstract

We establish discorrelation estimates between the Piatetski-Shapiro prime set \[ \mathcal{P}_γ := \{p \text{ is prime and } p = \lfloor n^{1/γ} \rfloor \text{ for some } n \in \mathbb{N}\} \] and arbitrary nilsequences when $γ\in (0,1)$ is sufficiently close to $1$. This extends earlier works which treated linear or polynomial exponential phase functions. As an application, we establish an asymptotic formula for the number of solutions in $\mathcal{P}_γ$ to any "finite-complexity" system of linear equations, including for the number of $k$-term arithmetic progressions in $\mathcal{P}_γ$ up to a threshold $N$ for any given $k \geq 3$. Furthermore, we show that there exists an absolute constant $C>0$ such that if \[ 1 - 2^{-Ck} < γ< 1, \] then the Piatetski-Shapiro primes $\mathcal{P}_γ$ contain infinitely many non-trivial $k$-term arithmetic progressions. This significantly improves upon the previous range of $γ$ obtained by Li and Pan, which is of triple exponential type.

Classicality of Hilbert modular forms

Yuanyang Jiang

The Fontaine-Mazur conjecture predicts that certain abstract Galois representations — symmetries of number fields — must come from classical modular forms (concrete analytic objects). This paper proves new cases of this conjecture for GL₂ over totally real number fields by showing that Hecke algebra characters appearing in completed cohomology are 'classical' when the associated Galois representation is irreducible and has regular de Rham (smoothness) properties. It generalizes techniques of Pan to the Hilbert modular setting.

math.NT
Original abstract

Let $F$ be a totally real number field. We prove that a character of the spherical Hecke algebra appearing in the completed cohomology of Hilbert modular varieties is modular if the associated Galois representation is absolutely irreducible, and de Rham of regular parallel weights. As an application, we prove some new cases of the Langlands-Clozel-Fontaine-Mazur conjecture of $\mathrm{GL}_2$ over totally real fields. For the proof, we generalize the method in [Pan26], calculate geometric partial Fontaine operators, and study the cohomology of the associated Koszul-type partial de Rham complexes. The key step is the establishment of a locally analytic Jacquet-Langlands transfer, whose proof consists of several novel ingredients including a comparison of Igusa stacks for different quaternionic Shimura data constructed by [DvHKZ26], and the Grothendieck-Messing theory for locally analytic infinite level Shimura varieties established in [Jiang26a].

Sum of consecutive powers as a perfect power

Angelos Koutsianas, Nikos Tzanakis

When can a sum of two consecutive k-th powers equal a perfect n-th power? This paper studies x^k + (x+1)^k = y^n for k ≡ 2 (mod 4) and n ≥ 3, proving the only solutions are trivial (x = 0 or −1) for k up to 100 or when k has odd prime factors congruent to 3 mod 4. The proof combines three powerful tools: linear forms in logarithms, the modular method (used in Fermat's Last Theorem), and Thue equations.

math.NT
Original abstract

In this paper we study the equation $$ x^k + (x+1)^k = y^n,\quad n\geq 3, $$ when $k\equiv 2\pmod{4}$. We prove that the only solutions are for $x=0, -1$ when $6\leq k\leq 100$ or for a $k$ with odd prime factors congruent to $3\pmod{4}$. We use linear forms in logarithms, the modular method and the resolution of Thue equations.

Non-admissibility of some universal supersingular representations

Zachary Feng, Heejong Lee, Ray Li et al.

Supersingular representations are mysterious mod-p representations of p-adic matrix groups that resist classification, and admissibility (having finite-dimensional fixed-point spaces) is a basic finiteness property. For GL_n over unramified p-adic fields with n≥3, this paper proves that universal supersingular representations are non-admissible and have infinite length under generic conditions, extending known n=2 results. This deepens the understanding of the wild, poorly-understood landscape of mod-p Langlands correspondences.

math.NT math.RT
Original abstract

Let $K/\mathbf{Q}_p$ be an unramified extension of degree $f$ with residue field $k$. Let $σ$ be an irreducible representation of $\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$ over $\overline{\mathbf{F}}_p$. For $n\ge 3$, we prove that the universal supersingular representation of weight $σ$ is non-admissible and of infinite length when $σ$ is sufficiently generic and satisfies certain technical conditions. This generalizes the previous results for $n=2$ and a non-trivial finite extension $K/\mathbf{Q}_p$. Our method employs a weight cycling argument together with recent progress on the Serre weight conjectures.

An update on the Linnik--Goldbach and Romanov problems

Daniel R. Johnston, Tim Trudgian

The Linnik-Goldbach problem asks how many powers of 2 are needed so that every large even integer equals two primes plus those powers. This paper shows that six powers of 2 suffice under the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, and unconditionally proves over 25% of odd numbers are a prime plus a power of 2—improving Romanov's constant. Both results push forward longstanding quantitative questions about additive representations involving primes.

math.NT
Original abstract

We consider the Linnik--Goldbach problem of writing all large even integers as the sum of two primes and a fixed number of powers of 2. We show that, under the generalised Riemann hypothesis, one can use 6 powers of two. In addition, we update the best known bounds on Romanov's constant, showing unconditionally that more than $25\%$ of odd numbers can be written as the sum of a prime and a power of 2.

Linear independence of periods related to polylogarithms

Makoto Kawashima

Polylogarithms are functions generalizing the logarithm, and their special values at algebraic numbers are mysterious constants whose arithmetic properties are largely unknown. This paper gives the first criteria proving that certain collections of these values are linearly independent over algebraic number fields, using explicitly constructed Padé-type approximants. This is a significant step toward understanding the algebraic relations—or lack thereof—among these fundamental constants.

math.NT
Original abstract

This paper provides the first criteria for the linear independence of multiple polylogarithm values over algebraic number fields. In particular, we derive novel results regarding the linear independence of products of polylogarithms at distinct points over an algebraic number field. Our approach is based on the explicit construction of Padé-type approximants tailored for multiple polylogarithms.

Boundedness of total Cartier indices for rational singularities in families

Jihao Liu, Ruicheng Hu, Sheng Qin

When varieties with rational singularities (mild geometric defects where certain cohomological data vanishes) come in bounded families, does a key numerical invariant stay controlled? This paper proves the total Cartier index—measuring how far a divisor is from being locally principal—remains bounded across such families, resolving a problem posed by Han and Jiang. Notably, the paper credits an AI system for originating the proof's overall structure.

Original abstract

We show that the total Cartier index of varieties with rational singularities in a bounded family is bounded. This solves a problem of Han and Jiang. Jihao Liu, Sheng Qin

Shokurov's global index conjecture predicts that the Cartier indices of canonical divisors on foliated varieties (spaces equipped with families of tangent directions) are universally bounded in low dimensions. This paper proves the conjecture for foliations on varieties of dimension at most three, answering an open question in the field. The authors note that part of the main result was obtained with AI assistance.

math.AG
Original abstract

A condensed proof of the pro-étale and étale exodromy theorems

Remy van Dobben de Bruyn

Exodromy theorems describe sheaves on a scheme by continuous functors from a Galois category—a structured record of how spaces decompose along algebraic strata. This paper gives a streamlined, self-contained proof of the pro-étale exodromy theorem by recasting the relevant Galois category in the language of condensed mathematics, which handles profinite topology more cleanly. The result makes a technically demanding area more accessible and recovers the classical étale version as a corollary.

math.AG math.CT
Original abstract

The exodromy correspondence of Barwick, Glasman, and Haine computes constructible sheaves of spaces on a scheme $X$ as an $\infty$-category of continuous functors from the profinite category $\operatorname{Gal}(X)$. Viewing $\operatorname{Gal}(X)$ instead as a condensed category, this was extended by Wolf to an exodromy correspondence for pro-étale sheaves. Using the condensed perspective from the outset, we give a quick and self-contained proof of the pro-étale exodromy theorem. This is used to extract an exodromy theorem for (Postnikov complete) étale sheaves that does not yet appear in the literature, which is closely related to Lurie's work on ultracategories. Finally, we use this to give a new proof of the constructible étale exodromy correspondence of Barwick, Glasman, and Haine. Without additional effort, our method removes the qcqs hypotheses on the schemes, and gives versions for sheaves with coefficients in more general $\infty$-categories. Finally, we refine the methods to obtain a $κ$-condensed statement whenever $κ> \lvert \mathcal O_X(U) \rvert$ for every affine open $U \subseteq X$.

Divisibility phenomena in motivic Bloch--Ogus theory

Jean-Louis Colliot-Thélène, Stefan Schreieder

How divisible are 'unramified' algebraic K-theory classes—those detecting no singular behavior along any codimension-one subvariety? For smooth projective varieties over separably closed or finite fields, this paper proves that unramified classes in Milnor K-groups are always divisible by any integer invertible in the base field, with an analogous result for motivic cohomology. This resolves a structural question about the relationship between geometric ramification conditions and divisibility in motivic invariants.

math.AG math.KT
Original abstract

Let X be a smooth projective variety over a field k. For k separably closed, we prove that the subgroup of unramified classes in the Milnor K-group $K^M_i(k(X))$ of the function field of X is contained in the subgroup of n-divisible elements of $K^M_i(k(X))$ for any integer n invertible in k. This generalizes to a statement for unramified motivic cohomology of arbitrary bidegree. We further show that whenever k is finite or separably closed and l is a prime invertible in k, then all but the last step in the Bloch--Ogus filtration of the motivic cohomology of X are l-divisible up to torsion. Generalizations of this last result to arbitrary quasi-projective k-schemes are also proven.

Ishii's conjecture and Bridgeland stability conditions for dihedral reflection groups

Shu Nimura

Ishii's conjecture predicts that for a finite group G acting on the complex plane, the G-equivariant Hilbert scheme resolves the quotient singularity in a specific way. This paper proves the conjecture for all dihedral reflection groups using Bridgeland stability conditions—parameters controlling which objects in a derived category count as 'stable'—by translating the problem via the McKay correspondence into a geometric construction on a root stack. The approach offers a new conceptual framework likely applicable beyond the dihedral case.

math.AG
Original abstract

We provide a new proof of Ishii's conjecture for any dihedral reflection group $G\subset GL_2(\mathbb{C})$ from the viewpoint of Bridgeland stability conditions. Our strategy is to reduce the problem, via the derived McKay correspondence, to a geometric construction of Bridgeland stability conditions on the root stack of the maximal resolution along the strict transform of the discriminant divisor.

Birational and $\mathbf{A}^1$-invariant lattices in the cohomology of the structure sheaf over non-archimedean fields

Alberto Merici, Kay Rülling, Shuji Saito

Given a smooth algebraic variety over a non-archimedean field (like p-adic numbers), can its sheaf cohomology be upgraded to a richer, more rigid structure? This paper shows the answer is yes: cohomology of the structure sheaf refines to an A¹-invariant theory valued in integral lattices. As a striking application, it proves that the automorphism group of a variety's function field is a birational invariant.

math.AG
Original abstract

We show that the cohomology of the structure sheaf of smooth and proper schemes over a complete non-archimedean field $K$ of characteristic zero, can be refined to an $\mathbf{A}^1$-invariant cohomology theory of smooth (not necessarily proper) schemes over $K$ with values in $\mathcal{O}_K$-lattices, and the same holds for $K$ of positive characteristic in dimensions at most $3$. As one application, we obtain that the automorphism group of the function field of a proper smooth variety $X$ of dimension at most 3 over a field of positive characteristic acts quasi-unipotently on the cohomology of the structure sheaf of $X$. The construction of the lattices relies on a variant of the tame cohomology of Hübner--Schmidt with coefficients in a twisted version of the tame structure sheaf and uses results from rigid analytic geometry on the cohomology of twisted integral rigid structure sheaves due to Bartenwerfer and van der Put.

The pro-étale fundamental group of singular schemes

Jiu-Kang Yu, Lei Zhang

The pro-étale fundamental group is an algebraic invariant capturing 'generalized covering space' data for a geometric object, extending the classical étale fundamental group to handle more exotic coverings. This paper computes it explicitly for a broad class of singular schemes by combining descent techniques with a van Kampen theorem for topological groups. The result generalizes known formulas for singular curves and characterizes when representations of this group factor through simpler ones.

math.AG math.AT math.NT
Original abstract

We compute the pro-étale fundamental group of a connected Nagata J-2 scheme in terms of the étale fundamental groups of the normalizations of its irreducible components and a discrete free group. The result generalizes a formula of E. Lavanda for semi-stable curves and relies on a combination of proper descent techniques for étale morphisms and a combinatorial van Kampen construction for Noohi groups. As a by-product we characterize when a continuous representation of the pro-étale fundamental group factors through a discrete quotient.

Motivic Galois theory for one-loop Feynman integrals in momentum space

Ulysse Mounoud

Feynman integrals, which compute quantum-mechanical scattering amplitudes, carry rich algebraic structure that motivic theory—a framework unifying cohomology theories—can capture precisely. This paper builds such a framework for one-loop Feynman integrals in momentum space, naturally handling graphs with cuts (corresponding to physical particles going on-shell), and computes the associated weight-graded structure in terms of explicit local systems over kinematic parameter space.

math.AG math.NT
Original abstract

We develop a motivic framework for Feynman integrals of one-loop graphs in momentum space. Its advantage compared to the already existing framework in Feynman representation is that it naturally includes graphs with cuts. To each such graph, we associate a motivic local system over the space of generic kinematics. Our construction is functorial with respect to the natural operations on graphs: edge contraction and cutting. We compute the weight-graded pieces of the motivic local systems. They are Tate twists of quadratic Artin motives associated with maximally cut quotient graphs. We also derive a formula for the (co)action of the de Rham motivic Galois group, expressed in terms of cut quotient graphs.

Zero cycles on Severi--Brauer flag varieties

Divyasree C-Ramachandran, Amit Hogadi

Severi–Brauer varieties are algebraic varieties that become projective space only after extending the base field, encoding the arithmetic complexity of central simple algebras. This paper pins down the torsion in the Chow group of zero-cycles—an algebraic invariant measuring algebraic cycles of dimension zero—on generalized Severi–Brauer varieties, proving it is controlled by the gcd of the index and the twist parameter, with several new cases where this group vanishes entirely.

math.AG
