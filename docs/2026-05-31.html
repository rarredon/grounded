Canonical extensions of $p$-adic shtukas on toroidal compactifications of Shimura varieties

Shengkai Mao, Peihang Wu

Shimura varieties are geometric objects central to modern number theory, and 'p-adic shtukas' are algebraic gadgets encoding arithmetic data. This paper extends these shtukas to the boundaries of compactified Shimura varieties—regions where the geometry degenerates—by introducing a new framework called 'log diamonds.' This matters because controlling boundary behavior is essential for applying these tools to longstanding problems in the Langlands program.

math.NT math.AG
Original abstract

We construct canonical extensions of $p$-adic shtukas on integral models of toroidal compactifications of abelian-type Shimura varieties with quasi-parahoric levels at any prime number $p$. More precisely, we define the notion of a log diamond as a $v$-sheaf associated with a log scheme over $\mathbb{Z}_p$ and construct a $p$-adic log shtuka over the log diamond of an integral toroidal compactification of an abelian-type Shimura variety by studying the ``degeneration'' of the shtuka at the boundary. Moreover, we provide a definition of canonical integral models of toroidal and minimal compactifications in the sense of Pappas and Rapoport, and verify it in the same generality as above. Applications include the canonicity and functoriality of integral toroidal compactifications, as well as an axiomatic proof of the well-positionedness of all well-known stratifications on the special fiber.

Iwasawa invariants of Bertolini--Darmon Theta Elements

Abhishek, Jishnu Ray, Pronay Kumar Karmakar

Iwasawa theory studies how arithmetic objects like ellipatic curves vary across infinite towers of number fields, encoded via algebraic invariants called μ and λ. This paper computes these invariants for Bertolini–Darmon theta elements — special p-adic L-functions attached to modular forms — in anticyclotomic extensions of imaginary quadratic fields, handling both ordinary and supersingular primes and extending previously known cyclotomic results.

math.NT
Original abstract

In this article we study the Iwasawa invariants of Bertolini--Darmon theta elements in the anticyclotomic $\mathbb{Z}_p$-extension of an imaginary quadratic field $K$ for weight two modular forms $f\in S_2(Γ_0(N))$. We cover both the cases of ordinary and non-ordinary reduction at a prime $p$. Our results extend the known results of Pollack--Weston and Leonard--Lei in the cyclotomic setting.

On a conjecture of Goldmakher

Alexander P. Mangerel

A completely multiplicative function assigns complex values to integers respecting multiplication. Goldmakher's 2009 conjecture predicted a precise bound on how large the logarithmically-averaged partial sums of such functions can get relative to a natural measure of their 'pretentious' behavior. This paper constructs an explicit counterexample, disproving the conjecture and showing the conjectured bound can fail dramatically.

math.NT
Original abstract

We construct a $1$-bounded completely multiplicative function $f$ whose logarithmically-averaged partial sums satisfy $$ \limsup_{x \rightarrow \infty} \frac{\left|\sum_{n \leq x} \frac{f(n)}{n}\right|}{1+\exp\left(\sum_{p \leq x} \frac{\text{Re}(f(p))}{p}\right)} = \infty. $$ This disproves a conjecture of Goldmakher from 2009.

The sum-product conjecture is false for real numbers

Thomas F Bloom, Will Sawin, Carl Schildkraut et al.

The Erdős–Szemerédi sum-product conjecture predicted that any finite set of real numbers must have either many pairwise sums or many pairwise products — specifically, that max(|A+A|, |AA|) grows nearly like |A|². This paper disproves it by constructing sets where both sums and products stay well below |A|², using algebraic integers from number fields of growing degree. It's a major surprise, overturning a central belief in additive combinatorics that stood for decades.

math.NT math.CO
Original abstract

We disprove the sum-product conjecture for real numbers by constructing arbitrarily large $A\subset \mathbb{R}$ (whose elements are algebraic integers in a number field of degree $\asymp \log\lvert A\rvert$) such that \[\max(\lvert A+A\rvert ,\lvert AA\rvert)\leq \lvert A\rvert^{2-c}\] where $c>0$ is an absolute constant. We also disprove the many sums and products conjecture by constructing, for any $k\geq 3$, arbitrarily large $A\subset \mathbb{R}$ such that \[\max(\lvert kA\rvert,\lvert A^{(k)}\rvert)\leq \lvert A\rvert^{C\frac{\log k}{\log\log k}}\] for some constant $C>0$. We obtain similar constructions for $p$-adics, finite fields, and function fields in positive characteristic, and also obtain new lower bounds for the number of solutions to linear equations in a multiplicative group and the number of solutions to the unit equation in sufficiently many variables.

Equidistribution of CM points and RM curves

Erick Ross, Hui Xue

CM points are special points in the upper half-plane encoding complex multiplication in number theory; Duke's 1988 theorem says they spread uniformly across the half-plane as their discriminant grows. This paper refines that picture, showing CM points and their positive-discriminant analogs (RM curves) also equidistribute when restricted to fixed geodesics or neighborhoods of fixed points—a finer-grained view of their arithmetic distribution.

math.NT
Original abstract

In 1988, William Duke showed that CM points of fundamental discriminant $D$ are equidistributed in the complex upper half-plane $\mathcal H$ as $D \to -\infty$. He also showed a similar result for RM curves (a positive discriminant analog of CM points). In this paper, we investigate analogous problems concerning the distribution of CM points and RM curves along fixed geodesics in $\mathcal H$, and around fixed points in $\mathcal H$. Specifically, we show that CM points and RM curves are equidistributed along every fixed rational geodesic in $\mathcal H$, and around every fixed CM point in $\mathcal H$. To prove these results, we solve the aggregate Linnik problem for arbitrary binary quadratic forms.

A unified proof of conjectures on the spaces of multiple $q$-zeta values

Minoru Hirose, Takumi Maesaka, Taiki Watanabe

Multiple q-zeta values are q-deformations of classical number-theoretic constants built from iterated sums, organized into nested vector spaces. This paper proves two conjectures by showing smaller, 'odd-indexed' generating families actually span the full spaces, and does so constructively with explicit integer-coefficient formulas. The unified approach resolves longstanding structural questions about these spaces and provides concrete tools for future computations.

math.NT
Original abstract

We prove two conjectures on the spaces generated by multiple $q$-zeta values. More precisely, we show that the spaces $Z_q^{\mathrm{o}}$ and $Z_{q,1}^{\mathrm{o}}$ already generate the larger spaces $Z_q$ and $Z_{q,1}$, respectively. Our result is stronger than the equality of $\mathbb{Q}$-vector spaces: for every generator in the larger spaces, we construct an explicit expression with integer coefficients in terms of the smaller generating families. We first establish these formulas at the finite level, where suitable finite $q$-analogues admit recursive descriptions through generating series, and then pass to the infinite limit.

Linnik's problem for multiplicative functions

Kaisa Matomäki, Joni Teräväinen

How long must you search through integers before finding sign changes in a multiplicative function? A multiplicative function assigns values to integers using prime factorizations. This paper proves that within any residue class mod q, sign changes always occur by q^(2+o(1)), unless the function's sign closely mimics a real Dirichlet character. This solves an analogue of Linnik's classical problem about the smallest prime in an arithmetic progression.

math.NT
Original abstract

We study a multiplicative function analogue of Linnik's problem on the least prime in an arithmetic progression. Let $h\colon \mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}$ be a multiplicative function, and let $a \pmod q$ be a reduced residue class. We ask how far one must go before finding square-free integers $n_1,n_2\equiv a \pmod q$ with $h(n_1)<0<h(n_2)$. We show that one can always find such integers with $n_1,n_2\le q^{2+o(1)}$, unless the sign of $h$ strongly pretends to be a real Dirichlet character modulo $q$. Thus, apart from this natural character obstruction, sign changes of a multiplicative function occur in every reduced residue class at a scale corresponding essentially to the square root barrier. In the special case of the Liouville function $λ$ this improves on a recent result of Ford and Radziwiłł and matches, up to $q^{o(1)}$ factors, what was previously known conditionally under the generalized Riemann hypothesis.

Inhomogeneous Approximation by Sums of Roots

Samuel Korsky

How closely can a fixed real number β be approximated by a sum of k d-th roots of integers up to N? This paper proves the approximation error can be made as small as N^(−k/d+ε), significantly improving the best previous bounds. The key tools are Schmidt's Subspace Theorem (a deep result in Diophantine geometry) combined with a new 'transference' argument linking homogeneous and inhomogeneous approximation problems.

math.NT math.CO
Original abstract

Let $d\geq 2$ and $k\geq 1$ be fixed. We prove that, for every $ε>0$ and every real $β$, there exist integers $1\leq b_1,\ldots,b_k\leq N$ such that \[ \left\|\sum_{j=1}^k b_j^{1/d}-β\right\| \ll_{d,k,ε} N^{-k/d+ε}. \] The proof combines Schmidt's Subspace Theorem with an explicit inhomogeneous transference argument. This improves Iyer's (2025) higher-root exponent $(k-d+1)/d^2$, and also the analogous $d$-ary full-basis exponent away from the cases where $k+1$ is a power of $d$, at the cost of ineffectivity. We also record a conjectural uniform exponent $k-1/d$. In the square-root case $d=2$, we give explicit integer-target constructions for $k=2,3,4$ attaining this conjectural value.

The Northcott Property for Composites of Number Fields of Bounded Degree

Benjamín Castillo

The Northcott property for a field extension means only finitely many algebraic numbers in it have bounded height (a measure of arithmetic complexity). This paper proves that infinite Galois extensions whose Galois group has finite exponent—meaning every element satisfies g^n = 1 for a fixed n—always have this property, using a structural theorem about profinite groups due to Segal.

math.NT
Original abstract

We prove that infinite Galois extensions of number fields with Galois group of finite exponent have the Northcott property. The main novelty of our approach lies in the application of a theorem of Segal on profinite groups.

Ekedahl-Oort strata under natural embeddings of orthogonal and unitary Shimura varieties

Yan Qijun, Zhang Chao

Shimura varieties are geometric objects encoding deep arithmetic information; Ekedahl-Oort (EO) strata partition their reductions modulo a prime p by the structure of p-torsion in associated abelian varieties. This paper tracks exactly how EO strata behave under natural embeddings between two flavors of Shimura varieties—orthogonal (GSpin) and unitary—relevant to the Kudla program of constructing special algebraic cycles. The results give concrete control over subtle mod-p geometry central to modern arithmetic geometry.

math.NT math.AG
Original abstract

In this paper, we study the behavior of Ekedahl-Oort strata under natural embeddings between the good reductions modulo $p$ of GSpin Shimura varieties and Rapoport-Smithling-Zhang unitary Shimura varieties, a prototypical setting for the construction of special cycles in the Kudla program. In each case, we determine the EO stratum containing the image of a given EO stratum under the embedding. We also compute discrete invariants of these Shimura varieties, including their $p$-ranks and $a$-numbers; in the GSpin case, these are obtained via the Kuga-Satake embedding.

Products involving the real parts of Jacobi sums and related cyclotomic matrices

Hai-Liang Wu, Xiao-Han Ji

Jacobi sums are finite-field analogues of the beta function, built from multiplicative characters of finite fields. This paper studies a specific product of real parts of Jacobi sums and reveals a surprising connection to determinants of cyclotomic matrices—matrices whose entries are quadratic characters evaluated at sums of elements. These links between analytic and algebraic objects deepen our understanding of multiplicative structure in finite fields.

Original abstract

Ultra Kolyvagin systems and higher Fitting ideals of Iwasawa Selmer groups

Kolyvagin systems are powerful tools for controlling Selmer groups—arithmetic objects encoding solutions to Diophantine equations—but break down over infinite coefficient rings like those in Iwasawa theory. By building 'ultra' versions using ultraproducts of classical Selmer groups, the paper computes the structure of Iwasawa Selmer groups up to pseudo-isomorphism and eliminates troublesome finite submodules, advancing the Iwasawa main conjecture program.

Connected components of the ranges of twisted divisor functions on number fields

Sophie Zhu

How spread out are the values of twisted divisor functions over number fields? For a number field K and Dirichlet character χ, the twisted ideal divisor function sums character-weighted powers of ideal norms over divisors. This paper counts how many connected pieces the closure of this function's range breaks into, generalizing classical results about ordinary divisor sums. The answer reveals deep structure in how arithmetic functions distribute their values across algebraic number fields.

math.NT
Original abstract

Let $r\in\mathbb{C}$, let $K$ be a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}$, let $I_K$ be the monoid of integral ideals in the ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_K$ of $K$, and let $χ$ be a Dirichlet character. Then define the twisted ideal divisor function $σ_{r, K, χ} : I_K \rightarrow \mathbb{C}$ by $$σ_{r,K,χ}(I) = \sum_{J \mid I} N(J)^{-r}χ(N(J)),$$ where $N$ denotes the ideal norm. For real $r>1,$ we study the number of connected components $C_{r, K, χ}$ of the closure $\overline{σ_{r,K,χ}(I_K)}$, writing $C_{r,K}$ when $χ$ is the principal character modulo 1. We prove that $C_{r,K,χ}$ is finite when $χ$ is real-valued. When $K = \mathbb{Q}$, we show that for fixed $r > 1,$ every sufficiently large positive integer is realized as $C_{r,\mathbb{Q},χ},$ and if $r$ is sufficiently large, then every positive integer is realized as $χ$ varies. For finite Galois extensions $K$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, we exhibit new exponential lower bounds for $C_{r,K},$ and we prove that for every fixed integer $s \geq 2$, the values $C_{r,K}$ are unbounded as $K$ ranges over degree-$s$ extensions of $\mathbb{Q}$.

A proof of the $4,7$ cases of Sylvester's conjecture on cube sums

Hongbo Yin

Sylvester conjectured in 1879 that every prime congruent to 4 or 7 mod 9 can be written as a sum of two rational cubes. This paper proves exactly that, resolving two-thirds of the conjecture by combining recent progress on the Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture for rank-0 elliptic curves with the newly proved Unbounded Denominators Conjecture—a 150-year-old number theory problem now largely settled.

math.NT
Original abstract

In this paper, we prove that every prime $p$ which is congruent to $4,7$ modulo $9$ is the sum of two rational cubes. This is $2/3$ of Sylvester's conjecture which has a history of nearly 150 years since 1879. In the proof, we use recent progress on Full BSD conjecture of rank $0$ elliptic curves in \cite{BF} to deduce that the Manin-Stevens constants of some families of elliptic curves are units. We also use recent solutions of Unbounded Denominators Conjecture in \cite{CDT} to prove that some cubic roots of modular functions are invariant under some congruence subgroups. Instead of using the Unbounded Denominators Conjecuture, we also give another conditional proof assuming the GRH for number fields or Artin's primitive root conjecture for arithmetic progressions.

Banded quadratic digit functions along irreducible polynomials over finite fields

Kaimin Cheng

This paper asks whether certain quadratic statistics on the coefficients of irreducible polynomials over finite fields are uniformly distributed — a finite-field analogue of asking whether primes behave randomly with respect to digit patterns. The answer is yes: the function Q_A is equidistributed over monic irreducible polynomials of degree n as n grows, extending classical results from linear to quadratic digit statistics and connecting to deep sieve methods in finite-field number theory.

math.NT
Original abstract

Let $q$ be an odd prime power and let $\F_q$ be the finite field with $q$ elements. Let $\mathcal{P}(n)$ be the set of monic irreducible polynomials of degree $n$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$. For $f=t^n+f_{n-1}t^{n-1}+\cdots+f_0\in\mathcal{P}(n)$, fix coefficients $c_0,\ldots,c_m\in\mathbb{F}_q$ with $c_m\ne0$ and put $$ Q_A(f)=\sum_{j=0}^m c_j\sum_{i=j}^n f_i f_{i-j}+\ell_n(f),$$ where $\ell_n$ is an arbitrary linear form in the coefficients of $f$ and $f_n=1$. We prove that $Q_A$ is equidistributed on $\mathcal{P}(n)$: for every $γ\in\mathbb{F}_q$, $$\#\{f\in\mathcal{P}(n):Q_A(f)=γ\}=\frac{\#\mathcal{P}(n)}{q}+O_A(q^{19n/20+o(n)}),$$ as \(n\to\infty\), with $q$ and the quadratic band fixed. This extends the finite-field Rudin--Shapiro result from nearest-neighbour correlations to arbitrary fixed symmetric Laurent symbols. The proof combines Vaughan's identity with rank estimates for Toeplitz forms; the main new ingredient is an averaged rank-defect estimate for reciprocal symbols in the central Type I range.

Lubin-Tate representations over nontrivial finite Galois extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_{p}$ are not Aut-intrinsically Hodge-Tate

Kaiji Kondo

Lubin-Tate formal groups are objects in p-adic number theory that produce explicit Galois representations—symmetries of number fields encoded in linear algebra. Being 'Hodge-Tate' is a key structural property of such representations, and 'Aut-intrinsically Hodge-Tate' asks whether this property is detectable from the group's automorphisms alone. This paper proves it isn't, for odd primes and nontrivial Galois base fields, completing a classification left open by earlier work.

math.NT
Original abstract

In the present paper, we show that, for an odd prime number $p$ and a nontrivial finite Galois extension $k$ of $\mathbb{Q}_{p}$, the $p$-adic representation of the absolute Galois group of $k$ determined by a Lubin-Tate formal group over the ring of integers of $k$ is not Aut-intrinsically Hodge-Tate [in the sense of Hoshi]. This settles the odd-degree cases left open in the previous works of Hoshi and the author and, together with the known even-degree case, completes the picture for finite Galois extensions of $\mathbb{Q}_{p}$ in the case where $p$ is odd. This exhibits a sharp contrast, from the viewpoint of anabelian geometry, between the $p$-adic cyclotomic character and other $p$-adic Lubin-Tate characters.

Simultaneous Periods for Families of Rational Maps Modulo Primes

Bhawesh Mishra

When you reduce a dynamical system modulo a prime, do periodic points stay non-periodic? This paper shows that for most finite sets of non-periodic points under rational maps over a number field, a positive-density set of primes preserves their non-periodicity simultaneously across multiple maps. This advances arithmetic dynamics — the study of iteration and periodicity in number-theoretic settings — by controlling how dynamical behavior behaves under reduction modulo primes.

math.NT math.CO
Original abstract

Let $K$ be a number field, and $\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{g}\in K(t)$ be finitely many rational maps, each of degree at least $2$. We first show that for generic finite sets $\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{g}$ consisting entirely of points that are not $\varphi_{i}$-periodic, there exists a set of primes $\mathfrak p$ of $K$ of positive density such that for each $\mathcal{A}_{i}$ and every $α\in\mathcal{A}_i$, $α$ is not $\varphi_i$-periodic modulo $\mathfrak p$. The notion of genericity used here is defined in terms of the associated arboreal fields and is sharper than those previously used in the literature. Leveraging our proof in the generic case, we then show that the same conclusion holds for most \textit{expected} cases of non-generic sets $\mathcal{A}_{i}$. Equality in a Reverse Minkowski Shell Bound for Integral Lattices via Spherical Designs

Scott Duke Kominers

How many lattice points can sit on a single sphere centered at the origin of an integer lattice? A known upper bound exists, and this paper completely classifies when equality holds: only the standard cubic lattice ℤⁿ (for shell radius 1) and the exceptional E₈ lattice (for radius √2) achieve it. The proof connects lattice geometry to spherical designs — configurations of points that integrate polynomials exactly — revealing deep rigidity.

math.NT cs.DM math.CO math.MG
Original abstract

For a full-rank integral lattice $\mathcal{L}\subset\mathbb{R}^n$, Regev and Stephens-Davidowitz proved that \[N_{=k}(\mathcal{L}):=|\{y\in\mathcal{L}:\lVert y\rVert^2=k\}|\le 2\binom{n+2k-2}{2k-1}.\] We classify the equality cases. For $n\ge2$, equality holds if and only if either $k=1$ and $\mathcal{L}\cong\mathbb{Z}^n$, or $n=8$, $k=2$, and $\mathcal{L}\cong E_8$. For $n=1$, equality holds exactly when $\mathcal{L}$ represents $k$. The proof shows that equality is rigid. Saturation of the shell bound forces the normalized norm-$k$ shell to be an antipodal tight spherical $(4k-1)$-design. The associated Delsarte--Goethals--Seidel annihilator polynomial gives an arithmetic root condition, which isolates $E_8$ at $k=2$, rules out $k=3$, and combines with the Bannai--Damerell/Bannai theorem and an elementary circle argument to exclude all remaining cases in dimension at least $2$.

A solution to the Yau-Tian-Donaldson Conjecture through Special Fujita Approximations

Antonio Trusiani

The Yau-Tian-Donaldson (YTD) conjecture asks: when does a complex manifold admit a canonical 'best' metric called a Kähler-Einstein metric? This paper resolves the uniform YTD conjecture by proving that any big line bundle admits a 'special Fujita approximation'—meaning its geometric invariants can be captured by simpler ample bundles—and uses this to tame a non-Archimedean entropy functional. We show that any big line bundle on a smooth projective variety admits a special Fujita approximation: the volume and the first Riemann-Roch coefficient are both approximated by those of ample $\mathbb{Q}$-line bundles on higher models. Georg Oberdieck, Rahul Pandharipande

Gromov-Witten invariants count curves on a geometric space; for K3 and abelian surfaces, computing these in 'imprimitive' curve classes—where curves wrap multiple times around a basic class—requires a multiple cover formula. This paper proves that two conjectured such formulas follow from a conjectural Gromov-Witten/Pairs correspondence for a broader class of 3-folds, reducing one hard open problem to another and clarifying the structure of curve-counting on these surfaces.

All reduced descendent Gromov-Witten invariants of $K3$ and abelian surfaces in primitive curve classes can be calculated by the methods of \cite{BOPY,MPT}. Complex abelian varieties and quantum error correction: a mathematical framework for GKP codes

Maxence Mayrand, Baptiste Royer

GKP codes are quantum error-correcting codes built from lattices in phase space, used in continuous-variable quantum computing. This paper reveals that these lattices naturally define polarized complex abelian varieties—geometric objects central to algebraic geometry—creating a precise dictionary between quantum coding theory and classical mathematics, potentially importing powerful geometric tools into the study of quantum error correction.

math.AG math-ph math.DG quant-ph
Original abstract

We study a class of quantum error-correcting codes through the geometry of complex abelian varieties. These codes, introduced by Gottesman--Kitaev--Preskill, are built from symplectically integral lattices and therefore naturally define polarized complex abelian varieties. We give a precise mathematical formulation of this relationship and extend it to a dictionary between the main structures of GKP code theory and classical objects in the theory of abelian varieties. For instance, under this dictionary, the finite-dimensional code space becomes the space of theta functions $H^0(X, L)$, logical Pauli gates arise from the theta group, passive logical Clifford gates correspond to automorphisms of the polarized abelian variety, and concatenation with stabilizer codes corresponds to isogeny. We also prove several key results that give precise mathematical formulations of statements about these codes that often appear in heuristic form in the physics literature. In particular, we prove that the encoding is asymptotically isometric, that every logical Clifford gate is realized by a Gaussian unitary, and that, for noise of small variance, the failure probability is governed to first order by the shortest nontrivial displacement in the kernel of the polarization isogeny, a systolic invariant of the underlying polarization. This leads naturally to optimization problems on the moduli space of polarized abelian varieties.

On the support of admissible subcategories
