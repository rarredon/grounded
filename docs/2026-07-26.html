A Weighted Sum Formula for Double Eisenstein Series

Henrik Bachmann

Double Eisenstein series are functions built from summing over pairs of lattice points, generalizing classical Eisenstein series in number theory. This paper proves a conjectured weighted sum formula relating these objects to generating series of multiple divisor sums—functions counting how integers factor. The result connects modular forms to combinatorial number theory via the double-shuffle relations governing multiple zeta values.

math.NT math.CO
Original abstract

We prove a weighted sum formula for double Eisenstein series. Its corresponding identity for the generating series of multiple divisor sums was conjectured by the author in his master's thesis. The double Eisenstein series identity follows from the restricted double-shuffle relations proved by the author and Tasaka, while the proof of the divisor-sum identity is combinatorial and uses generating series.

Zeta functions of $\mathrm{PGL}_n$ over non-Archimedean local fields

Ming-Hsuan Kang, Jiu-Kang Yu

How do you count closed loops in exotic geometric spaces? This paper defines and studies zeta functions for quotients of the Bruhat–Tits building of PGL_n over local fields (think p-adic analogues of symmetric spaces), proving that two natural notions of 'geodesic' coincide. This makes the zeta function computable locally and yields rationality results generalizing classical graph-theoretic zeta functions to higher-dimensional simplicial complexes.

math.NT
Original abstract

Let $\mathscr{B}$ be the Bruhat--Tits building of $\mathrm{PGL}_n(F)$, where $F$ is a non-Archimedean local field. We introduce geometric $k$-geodesics in $\mathscr{B}$ by means of CAT(0) convexity and combinatorial $k$-geodesics by a local successor relation on pointed $k$-facets. We prove that the two notions coincide. This allows us to use the local combinatorial definition on quotients $Γ\backslash\mathscr{B}$, without referring to the universal covering. When $Γ$ is discrete, torsion-free, cocompact, and type-preserving, the primitive closed $k$-geodesics define zeta functions $Z_k$ and their $ε$-twisted variants $Z_k^ε$. Our main result identifies an alternating product of these zeta functions with the unramified $L$-function of $L^2(Γ\backslash \mathrm{PGL}_n(F))$: $(1-u^n)^{χ(Γ\backslash\mathscr{B})}L(Γ,q^{(n-1)/2}u)=\prod_{k=1}^{n-1} Z_k^ε(Γ\backslash\mathscr{B},u)^{(-1)^{k+1}}$. This gives a uniform Ihara-type identity for all $\mathrm{PGL}_n$. We also extend the construction and the identity to $\mathrm{PGL}_n(D)$, where $D$ is a central division algebra over $F$; in that setting the residue parameter is $Q=|\mathcal{O}_D/\mathfrak{p}_D|$.

Murmurations of quadratic Hecke $L$-functions of the Gaussian field

Peng Gao, Liangyi Zhao

Murmurations are recently discovered oscillatory patterns in the average values of L-functions — number-theoretic objects encoding deep arithmetic information — when sorted by conductor. This paper computes the precise 'murmuration density' for quadratic Hecke L-functions over the Gaussian integers, assuming the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, giving an explicit formula governing these mysterious oscillations in a new setting.

math.NT
Original abstract

In this paper, we evaluate the murmuration density for the family of quadratic Hecke $L$-functions of the Gaussian field under the generalized Riemann hypothesis.

The Pila-Zannier strategy for Drinfeld modules and Drinfeld modular curves

Gal Binyamini, Dmitry Novikov, Francesco Maria Saettone

Can classical results about special points on algebraic curves be transplanted to the world of function fields? This paper extends the Pila-Zannier method—a powerful strategy combining logic and number theory—to Drinfeld modules, proving function-field analogues of the Manin-Mumford and André-Oort theorems. The key innovation replaces o-minimality (a real-analytic tool unavailable over function fields) with a rigid analytic counting theorem, marking its first arithmetic application.

math.NT math.AG
Original abstract

We extend the Pila-Zannier strategy to Drinfeld modules: we prove analogues of the Manin-Mumford theorem for a product of two Drinfeld modules of equal rank, and of the André-Oort theorem for a product of two Drinfeld modular curves. In characteristic zero, several steps of this strategy rest on $o$-minimality, which has no counterpart over a function field; we replace the counting step by the rigid analytic Pila-Wilkie theorem of Binyamini-Kato, and this appears to be its first arithmetic application. The functional transcendence input, namely an analogue of the Ax-Lindemann theorem in both settings, is established here by an independent point counting argument.

On the $\mathcal{A}$-transcendence of a Champernowne-type constant

Shin-ichiro Seki

Can a polynomial in the integers avoid being divisible by arbitrarily many primes placed at prime-indexed positions? This paper proves that for any nonzero integer polynomial f, infinitely many primes p_n fail to divide f(n)—a result connected to transcendence properties of Champernowne-type constants built by concatenating prime values. It confirms these constants can't satisfy simple algebraic relations over the integers.

math.NT
Original abstract

Let $p_n$ denote the $n$-th prime. We prove that for every nonzero polynomial $f(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]$, there exist infinitely many positive integers $n$ such that $p_n\nmid f(n)$.

Exploiting Load/Store Leakage of Sparse Vectors for Key Recovery in HQC

Gustavo Banegas, Benjamin Smith, Jad Zahreddine

HQC is a post-quantum encryption scheme recently chosen for NIST standardization, designed to resist attacks from quantum computers. This paper demonstrates a side-channel attack—exploiting physical information leaked during computation rather than breaking the math—that recovers HQC's secret key by observing how a processor's memory operations handle sparse vectors differently depending on data values. The attack threatens real-world implementations even though the underlying mathematics remains sound.

cs.CR math.NT
Original abstract

Hamming Quasi-Cyclic (HQC) is a code-based key encapsulation mechanism selected by NIST for standardization, making its resistance to implementation attacks critically important. We present a side-channel attack that exploits load/store leakage in the manipulation of HQC's sparse secret vectors. Analysing Cortex-M4 assembly generated from the reference implementation, we identify a leakage surface in which the low and high 32-bit halves of each 64-bit word leak with different strengths, due to compiler-generated register spilling. We exploit this leakage to construct a simple zero-word distinguisher classifying machine words of the secret vector as zero or nonzero from electromagnetic measurements. The recovered zero positions are then translated into decoding hints, reducing HQC key recovery to a shortened syndrome-decoding problem. We analyse the resulting decoding complexity for all HQC parameter sets: at 32-bit granularity an expected 88.7% of the machine words of y are zero for HQC-1, cutting the decoding to $\approx$ 2 46 bit operations. Experiments on a Cortex-M4 validate the predicted low/high-half asymmetry-approximately 500 traces for the stronger low-half channel and 5,000 for the weaker high-half channeland recover the zero words of an HQC-1 key at 32-bit granularity. Finally, we discuss practical countermeasures that eliminate the sparsity exploited by the attack.

On the digits of the sum of proper divisors

Kübra Benli, Cécile Dartyge, Charlotte Dombrowsky et al.

How random-looking are the digits of s(n), the sum of a number's proper divisors? This paper proves s(n) follows Benford's law (leading digits skew toward 1) and that almost all integers have every decimal digit appearing in both the first and last k(x) digits of s(n) for any growing function k(x). These results reveal surprisingly uniform digit behavior in a number-theoretic function tied to the deep, unsolved problem of perfect numbers.

math.NT
Original abstract

We study several probabilistic questions concerning the digits of $s(n)$, the sum of proper divisors of an integer $n$. In particular, we show that $s(n)$ obeys Benford's law with respect to logarithmic density. Moreover, we show that, for every function $k(x) \rightarrow \infty$, almost all integers $n \leq x$ have every decimal digit occurring among the first $k(x)$ digits and the last $k(x)$ digits of $s(n)$. We also present an upper bound for the number of composite integers $n$ up to $x$ for which $s(n)$ is missing at least one digit in its decimal expansion. This is in contrast with the main result of a recent paper of Benli, Cesana, Dartyge, Dombrowsky, and Thompson, in which the inputs $n$ were not required to be composite. It turns out that the primes make a substantial contribution to the preimage set $s^{-1}(\mathcal{A})$, where $\mathcal{A}$ is a set of integers with missing digits. Our result for composite $n$ shows that the count is much smaller when prime inputs are excluded.

Unlocking the Wronskian Tower: A Simplification of the Holomorphic Modular Bootstrap

Arpit Das, Sunil Mukhi

Classifying rational conformal field theories (CFTs)—quantum field theories with finitely many particle types—requires solving modular linear differential equations (MLDEs), but this becomes intractable at high Wronskian index ℓ due to accessory parameters. This paper introduces differential operators linking high-ℓ solutions to simpler low-ℓ ones, dramatically simplifying the classification. The result unlocks previously inaccessible regions of CFT space and streamlines the holomorphic modular bootstrap program.

hep-th math-ph math.NT math.QA math.RT
Original abstract

Characters of rational conformal field theories solve modular linear differential equations labelled by their order and the Wronskian index $\ell$. Direct classification of admissible solutions by solving MLDEs becomes increasingly difficult at higher $\ell$ -- where movable poles and accessory parameters appear. In this work we introduce differential operators that relate higher-$\ell$ solutions to lower-$\ell$ ones while preserving modular covariance and integrality of the \(q\)-series. In rank two, this generates all allowed Wronskian sectors from the Mathur--Mukhi--Sen equation. In rank three and higher, it reduces the construction of higher-$\ell$ quasi-characters to simpler equations with lower $\ell$. This gives an efficient new route for organising candidate RCFT characters, and more generally quasi-characters, across the Wronskian tower. As an application, we apply our construction to prove a previously conjectured property on the signs of $\ell=2$ quasi-characters in rank 2.

Betti-Whittaker periods under duality: variations and applications

Giancarlo Castellano, Shih-Yu Chen, Nasit Darshan et al.

Betti-Whittaker periods are numerical invariants attached to automorphic representations—functions on arithmetic groups central to the Langlands program—and understanding how they behave under duality is important for connecting them to L-functions. This paper generalizes a previous result to GL_n over any number field without restrictive regularity assumptions, and crucially removes the need to invoke L-values in the proof, giving a cleaner and more broadly applicable framework.

math.NT
Original abstract

One of the authors (Chen) had previously proved a result on the behavior of Betti-Whittaker periods under duality for cohomological cuspidal automorphic representations of ${\rm GL}_n/{\mathbb Q}$ under some regularity assumptions while using their relation to $L$-values as an anchor in his proof. In this article we prove a generalization of this result to ${\rm GL}_n$ over any number field $F$ without any regularity assumptions and without recourse to $L$-values, while using the outer-automorphism of ${\rm GL}_n$ as the main tool. Then, using results of Harder and one of the other authors (Raghuram), we give applications to new rationality results for the ratios of special values of general triple product $L$-functions and for general twisted Asai $L$-functions. We also give a new proof of a previous result of Bhagwat and Raghuram on the special values of $L$-functions for orthogonal groups. We present variations on period relations for the Betti-Shalika periods under duality, and the behavior of Betti-Whittaker periods under Galois automorphisms of $F$.

Diophantine analysis and the Braid group ${\bf B}_3$

Wei He, Wenhao Lu, Hang Yang et al.

What number-theoretic properties do algebraic varieties built from group representations carry? Focusing on the braid group B₃ — which encodes the topology of three strands weaving around each other — this paper analyzes 'eigensurfaces,' the zero sets of characteristic polynomials attached to representations, using Diophantine methods that study integer or rational solutions to polynomial equations. The results reveal deep arithmetic structure hidden inside these representation-theoretic objects.

math.NT
Original abstract

Given a finite dimensional representation $π$ of a finitely generated group $G=\langle g_1, \ldots, g_n\rangle$, the associated characteristic polynomial is defined as $Q_π(z):=\det(z_0I+z_1π(g_1)+\cdots +z_nπ(g_n))$, and it is known to contain a good amount of structural information about $G$ and $π$. This paper is a part of an ongoing project to investigate the number-theoretic properties of the algebraic varieties (called {\em eigensurfaces}) $\{z\in \mathbb{C}^{n+1}: Q_π(z)=0\}$. Its focus is the distribution of prime triples in the eigensurface $S:=\{z\in \mathbb{C}^3: (z_0+z_1+z_2)^2+z_0z_1=0\}$ associated with the braid group ${\bf B}_3$ and its reduced Burau representation. We prove that such triples occur with higher frequency on $S$ than in the ambient lattice, revealing an unexpected connection between group representation theory and analytic number theory.

Quadratic residue patterns of length 4 and 5

Abdulkadyr Buchaev, Michael Tsfasman

Among consecutive integers modulo a prime, which patterns of quadratic residues (perfect squares) and non-residues actually occur, and how often? The authors extend previous work to all patterns of length 4 and 5, giving exact formulas via counts of points on hyperelliptic curves — low-genus algebraic curves defined by equations like y²=f(x). This resolves which patterns are realizable and quantifies their frequency with precision unavailable for longer lengths.

math.NT math.AG
Original abstract

We generalize the results of the first part of the paper by Kiritchenko, Tsfasman, Vlăduţ and Zakharevich on quadratic residue patterns to the case of arbitrary words over the alphabet $\{R, N\}$ of length $l = 4$ and $l = 5$. First, we obtain explicit formulas for the number of occurrences of a given pattern $S$ in the sequence of quadratic residues and nonresidues modulo a prime $p$. These formulas are expressed in terms of Frobenius traces on a finite collection of low-genus hyperelliptic curves. Second, using a generalized Sato-Tate approach, we describe the limiting distributions of the normalized error term for $l = 4$.

On Erdos-Falconer distance problem in even dimensions

Thang Pham, Chun-Yen Shen, Boqing Xue

The Erdős-Falconer distance problem asks: how large must a subset of a finite field's d-dimensional space be to guarantee it spans a positive proportion of all possible distances? The authors show that the conjecture in any even dimension reduces to the harder planar (2D) case, then use this to set new records for the required size threshold. This dimensional reduction is a structural breakthrough that concentrates future effort on a single foundational case.

math.NT math.CA math.CO
Original abstract

Let $q$ be an odd prime power and $\mathbb{F}_q$ be the finite field of order $q$. We prove an extraction theorem for the Erdős-Falconer distance conjecture in even dimensions, showing that the conjecture for all even dimensions reduces to the planar case. As consequences, we obtain improved thresholds on the pinned distance problem and the distribution of triangles, achieving new records of $\frac{d}{2}+\frac{1}{4}$ over prime fields and $\frac{d+1}{2}+\frac{1}{10}$ over arbitrary finite fields, respectively.

Quantum index, Arnold-Rokhlin surfaces, and real enumerative geometry

Ilia Itenberg, Eugenii Shustin

Two different sign-counting rules for real algebraic curves on toric surfaces turn out to be secretly equivalent. This paper reconciles Mikhalkin's quantum index with another Welschinger-type rule, clarifying the relationship between tropical geometry and real enumerative invariants, and proposes a new, more broadly applicable definition of quantum index useful for refined curve counting.

math.AG
Original abstract

The main goal of this note is to relate two different Welschinger-type rules of signs that were used for definition of real enumerative invariants of toric surfaces (the invariants considered being relative to the toric boundary). The relation between them intertwines Mikhalkin's quantum index and geometry of real and complex point sets of counted real curves. As a by-product, we suggest a new definition of quantum index, which can be used for refined invariant enumeration of real algebraic curves on surfaces in a non-toric setup.

Generic ordinarity for abelian coverings of the projective line

Régis Blache

Algebraic curves in characteristic p can be classified by their 'Newton polygon,' a numerical invariant measuring p-adic properties. This paper shows that abelian covers of the projective line (curves built from symmetric symmetry groups with order coprime to p) are generically 'μ-ordinary'—meaning they achieve the most generic possible Newton polygon. The result settles the generic behavior for a broad class of Hurwitz spaces by connecting them to well-understood Shimura varieties.

math.AG math.NT
Original abstract

We show that abelian coverings of the projective line of order prime to $p$ are generically $μ$-ordinary in characteristic $p$. The images of the irreducible components of Hurwitz spaces of abelian coverings of the projective line by the Torelli morphism lie in some Shimura varieties. The stratification by Newton polygons of these varieties is known, and we show that the generic Newton polygon for the Hurwitz space coincides with the generic (or $μ$-ordinary) Newton polygon of the smallest Shimura variety that contains its image. In order to do this, we compute the generic Newton polygons for $L$-functions associated to multiplicative character sums over the projective line.

Absolute minimum of a height associated to a semipositive adelic line bundle

Shu Kawaguchi, Fabien Pazuki

In arithmetic geometry, the 'height' of a point measures arithmetic complexity, and algebraic points were expected to achieve the minimum height for well-behaved (semipositive adelic) line bundles. This paper constructs an explicit counterexample over the rationals where the minimum height exists but is not achieved by any algebraic point, using dynamical systems on the projective line.

math.AG math.DS math.NT
Original abstract

There exists a semipositive adelic line bundle on a projective variety over $\mathbb{Q}$ whose absolute minimum does not attain the minimum by algebraic points. The construction is based on arithmetic properties of a family of dynamical systems over $\mathbb{P}^1$.

Stacky Framework for the Classification of Singular Fibers in Fibered Surfaces

Javier Reyes

How do you systematically classify the ways a smooth surface can develop singular fibers over a curve? This paper uses algebraic stacks—geometric objects that track symmetries more carefully than ordinary spaces—to reframe fibrations of genus-g curves as maps into a moduli stack, giving a clean independent verification of Dokchitser's classification of fiber reduction types. The approach works whenever the characteristic doesn't divide small integers (the 'tame' case).

math.AG
Original abstract

We develop a strategy for the classification of singular fibers in smooth fibered surfaces by exploiting the fact that, in the tame case, a fibration with smooth general fiber of genus $g > 1$ corresponds to a representable morphism from a stacky base to $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_g$ thanks to the root stack valuative criterion for properness of tame stacks. This framework serves as another justification of Dokchitser's classification of reduction types independently of Winters' abstract result.

Isotrivial smooth curves on surfaces

Xi Chen, Frank Gounelas

Can a 'nice' algebraic surface be completely covered by a family of curves that are all copies of the same curve? This paper proves the answer is no for non-uniruled surfaces of Picard rank one — surfaces with essentially one line bundle controlling geometry. It rules out isotrivial (constant-fiber) curve families in the natural polarization, constraining how such surfaces can be swept out.

math.AG

We prove that a smooth projective non-uniruled surface of Picard rank one, generated by an ample and base point free line bundle, cannot be covered by an isotrivial family of smooth curves in the primitive polarisation.

Basic properties of kappa classes

Valery Alexeev

Kappa classes are cohomological invariants on moduli spaces — parameter spaces tracking families of geometric objects — originally defined for curves. This paper extends them to KSBA moduli stacks, which parametrize higher-dimensional stable varieties, proving key structural properties like base-change compatibility and a vanishing theorem tying nontriviality to a quantity called normalized variation.

math.AG
Original abstract

We define kappa classes on KSBA moduli stacks as classes in operational Chow cohomology. They generalize the Miller-Morita-Mumford classes on the moduli spaces of curves. We prove base-change compatibility, product and normalization formulas, and crepant functoriality, together with the vanishing of all kappa polynomials above the variation. The higher kappa classes are nonnegative on effective cycles, and the largest index of a numerically nontrivial kappa class equals the normalized variation, while the first kappa class detects the total variation. As the boundary coefficients vary, the classes are chamberwise polynomial and compatible under operational wall crossing. Finally, every positive-degree kappa class is a rational multiple of a single Chern class of a natural virtual vector bundle.

Stringy Hodge numbers are invariants that generalize classical Hodge numbers to singular algebraic varieties, and Batyrev conjectured they are always nonnegative — a cornerstone belief motivating work in mirror symmetry and motivic integration for over two decades. This paper disproves that conjecture with an explicit counterexample, notably discovered with assistance from ChatGPT, fundamentally reshaping expectations in this area.

math.AG
Original abstract

Batyrev's conjecture on the non-negativity of stringy Hodge numbers has been a fundamental open problem, guiding and motivating many beautiful mathematical results in motivic integration, mirror symmetry, and the McKay correspondence. We give a counter-example to Batyrev's conjecture which was discovered with the assistance of OpenAI's ChatGPT.

Zero-cycles on surfaces dominated by products of hyperelliptic curves

Jean-Louis Colliot-Thélène, Federico Scavia, Alexei Skorobogatov

This paper asks whether a surface defined by y² = f₁(x₁)f₂(x₂) has a degree-1 zero-cycle (a formal combination of points with total degree 1) whenever it has points over every local field — a 'local-to-global' principle. Assuming finiteness of certain Tate-Shafarevich groups (which measure the failure of local-global principles for elliptic curves), the authors prove this holds for algebraically general polynomials f₁, f₂. The proof creatively combines fibration techniques with parity results for Selmer groups in quadratic twist families.

math.AG math.NT
Original abstract

Conditionally on the finiteness of the relevant Tate-Shafarevich groups, we prove a local-to-global result for zero-cycles of degree $1$ on the surfaces given by $y^2 = f_1(x_1)f_2(x_2)$, where the polynomials $f_1$ and $f_2$ are algebraically general. The proof combines the fibration method, parity results for $2$-Selmer groups in quadratic twist families, and a variant of a theorem of Morgan on the variation of the Cassels-Tate pairing.

Semipositivity of the orbifold second Chern class in Fujiki's class

Masataka Iwai, Satoshi Jinnouchi, Shiyu Zhang

Chern classes are numerical invariants measuring the curvature of a complex manifold; Miyaoka's inequality is a fundamental constraint on these invariants for varieties with nef (numerically effective, roughly 'non-negatively curved') canonical divisors. This paper extends these inequalities to singular complex varieties in Fujiki's class — a broad category generalizing compact Kähler manifolds — proving both Miyaoka's inequality and semipositivity of the orbifold second Chern class. The key technical tools are new generic nefness theorems and an orbifold Bogomolov–Gieseker inequality.

math.AG math.CV math.DG
Original abstract

We study inequalities for orbifold second Chern classes of compact normal analytic varieties in Fujiki's class. We prove Miyaoka's inequality for singular varieties in Fujiki's class with nef canonical divisor, as well as the semipositivity of the orbifold second Chern class for varieties with nef anti-canonical divisor. To prove these results, we establish generic nefness theorems for tangent and cotangent sheaves and an orbifold Bogomolov--Gieseker inequality for mixed polarizations.

On the Toda hierarchy for the stationary theory of the tube with negative contact orders

Hsian-Hua Tseng

The Toda hierarchy is an integrable system whose solutions, called tau functions, encode deep enumerative geometry. This paper constructs an explicit tau sequence governing the stationary Gromov–Witten theory of the projective line with prescribed negative contact orders at two points, using infinite-wedge algebra machinery. The result extends the Toda hierarchy framework to a previously inaccessible regime of contact conditions, connecting combinatorics of partitions to curve-counting invariants.

math.AG
Original abstract

We construct a master $2$-Toda tau sequence for the stationary relative theory of $(\PP^1,0,\infty)$ with any fixed finite lists of negative contacts. The negative-contact blocks are encoded by finite products of exponentials of infinite-wedge Lie algebra elements, with the block at $\infty$ taken as the adjoint of the entire corresponding left block. After adjoining independent positive-profile Toda times, the resulting charged-vacuum matrix elements form a genuine $2$-Toda tau sequence. Following the standard $Q,u$ specialization, its positive signed-degree coefficients recover the stationary relative Gromov--Witten invariants with the prescribed positive and negative contacts, which occur in and are constrained by the induced coefficientwise Hirota equations. The master tau sequence is larger than the enumerative sector: it generally contains nonzero, non-vacuum signed-degree-zero coefficients, as well as auxiliary nonzero lattice components, for which no enumerative interpretation is asserted. Consequently the Hirota equations form a coupled master system and do not, without additional input, close on the positive-degree invariants alone. The construction uses the large-root definition of negative contact, the Johnson--Kumaran--Wu operator formula for the tube, and the Kyoto/Plücker construction of the $2$-Toda hierarchy.

Homological rigidity and Schur rigidity of Schubert varieties in rational homogeneous spaces

Cong Ding, Qifeng Li

Schubert varieties are special subvarieties of flag-like spaces G/P that encode combinatorial data about Lie groups. This paper classifies which Schubert varieties are 'homologically rigid' (any homologous subvariety must be a translate) or 'Schur rigid' (multiples of the class decompose into translates), settling longstanding questions about when topology alone forces geometric rigidity in these highly symmetric spaces.

math.AG math.CV
Original abstract

A Schubert variety $X_0$ on a rational homogenous space $X=G/P$ is said to be homologically rigid, if any subvariety $Z$ on $X$ representing the same homology class with $X_0$ must satisfy $Z=g\cdot X_0$ for some $g\in{\rm Aut_0}(X)$. We say $X_0$ is Schur rigid, if furthermore any subvariety $Z$ on $X$ whose homology class is a multiple $r$ of that of $X_0$ must satisfy $Z=g_1\cdot X_0+\cdots+g_r\cdot X_0$ for some $g_1,\cdots ,g_r\in{\rm Aut_0}(X)$. Homological rigidity and Schur rigidity of Schubert varieties in rational homogeneous spaces of Picard number one have been well studied in extensive literature. In this paper, we study both rigidity problems of Schubert varieties in rational homogeneous spaces of higher Picard numbers. We show that in the long root cases, including all cases when $G$ is of type $ADE$, smooth Schubert varieties have homological rigidity. Besides, we give the complete list of Schubert varieties of subdiagram type with/without homological rigidity. Furthermore, for a Schubert variety $X_0$ of subdiagram type, we show that it has Schur rigidity in long root cases unless $X_0$ admits a fiber bundle structure over the projective space.

Foliations with small singular set in arbitrary characteristic

Thiago Fassarella, Wodson Mendson, João Pedro dos Santos et al.

Foliations—geometric structures decomposing a variety into lower-dimensional 'leaves'—behave differently in positive characteristic than over the complex numbers. This paper classifies regular foliations on key surface types and identifies where characteristic-zero intuition breaks down, clarifying the landscape of foliation geometry across all characteristics.

math.AG
Original abstract

This paper investigates the geometry of foliations on smooth algebraic varieties over an algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic $p \ge 0$. We address several specific features of foliations in positive characteristic, aiming to highlight both similarities and differences with the characteristic zero case. First, we provide a complete classification of regular foliations on minimal rational and del Pezzo surfaces, establishing that in positive characteristic, such foliations are $p$-closed. However, there are regular foliations on weak del Pezzo surfaces, in characteristic 2, which are not $p$-closed. We extend the Camacho-Sad index and its associated sum formula to arbitrary characteristic, using it to study the behavior of foliations and distributions with small singular set. For foliations on projective spaces, we prove that the existence of an invariant hypersurface with a sufficiently small singular set forces the foliation to be $p$-closed and imposes strict divisibility conditions on the degree of its normal bundle. Finally, we establish versions of the Bott vanishing theorem in both Hodge cohomology and the Chow ring for $p$-closed foliations, providing a positive characteristic analogue to classical vanishing results in complex geometry.

Obstructions to embedding singular curves in toric varieties

Maya Banks, Izzet Coskun, Kevin Tucker

Can a singular curve that fits inside projective space always be embedded in a simpler toric variety of lower dimension? This paper says no: for every d ≥ 2, there exist irreducible curves embeddable in d-dimensional projective space but in no projective normal toric variety of smaller dimension—including no lower-dimensional weighted projective space. This settles a natural question about the limits of toric geometry as a simplifying framework for curve embeddings.

math.AG math.AC
Original abstract

For every integer $d \geq 2$, we show that there exists an irreducible, reduced curve which embeds in $\mathbb{P}^d$ but in no projective normal toric variety of dimension less than $d$. In particular, there exist reduced irreducible curves that embed in $\mathbb{P}^d$ but do not embed in any weighted projective space of dimension less than $d$.

Osama Khlaif, Boris Pioline, Alessandro Tanzini

Can physical tools from 5D supersymmetric gauge theory produce refined mathematical invariants counting sheaves on surfaces? This bridges supersymmetric physics and enumerative geometry, producing new formulas for these difficult-to-compute topological invariants.

This paper strengthens the theorem by adding a matching lower bound on how the skeleton measure fills balls, tightening the correspondence and improving the distortion constant to O(1/ε).

Original abstract

The ultrametric skeleton theorem associates with every compact metric probability space $(X,d,μ)$ a compact subset $S\subseteq X$ of ultrametric distortion $O(1/\varepsilon)$ and a probability measure $ν$ supported on $S$ such that $ν(B_d(x,r))\leqμ(B_d(x,C_\varepsilon r))^{1-\varepsilon}$. We prove a stronger version with a compatible lower estimate on $ν(B_d(x,r))$ and with $C_\varepsilon=O(1/\varepsilon)$.

Gromov-Hausdorff distance and Jung constant of finite-dimensional normed spaces

Henry Adams, Semeon A. Bogatyi, Florian Frick et al.

The Gromov-Hausdorff distance measures how far apart two metric spaces are as abstract shapes; how does it relate to the simpler Hausdorff distance inside a normed space? This paper proves a lower bound: the Gromov-Hausdorff distance between a normed space and a nearby subset is at least the Hausdorff distance divided by twice the Jung constant—a number capturing how efficiently balls can cover bounded sets. This gives a concrete geometric handle on an otherwise hard-to-compute global distance.

Original abstract

For a finite-dimensional normed space $V$ and a subset $X$ with finite Hausdorff distance from $V$, we prove that the Gromov--Hausdorff distance between $X$ and $V$ is at least the Hausdorff distance between $X$ and $V$, divided by twice the relative Jung constant of $V$. If $V$ furthermore satisfies a certain intersection property, we show a stronger result where the relative Jung constant can be replaced with its absolute version. Key words: Normed spaces, Jung constant, Hausdorff distance, Gromov--Hausdorff distance.
